## Notes

- The USS Nimitz 5,370 g figure derives from Knuth et al. (2019) modeling of witness-reported SPY-1 parameters (descent from ~28,000 ft to ~50 ft in ~0.78 s, assuming the object starts and ends approximately at rest — an accelerate-then-decelerate profile); detailed radar data were not available to the authors. The result is a modal 5,370 (+1,430/−820) g under that model and is sensitive to the assumed descent time and velocity profile (see Section 2.2 sensitivity analysis). It is a low-confidence, source-dependent scenario, not a measurement.
- The Five Observables taxonomy was popularized by Luis Elizondo, who states that he directed AATIP (a role the DoD has publicly disputed); it is not a DoD, ODNI, or AARO classification system.
- A point-mass estimate requires ~1.6 × 10²²–1.6 × 10²⁴ kg (1.5 × 10³⁹–1.5 × 10⁴¹ J) for a 1″ deflection at b = 10 m–1 km; extended warp geometries require ray tracing.
- The NASA UAP Independent Study Team (2023) estimated that the GoFast object was at ~13,000 ft moving at roughly 40 mph (wind-like), with residual uncertainty because wind effects on the aircraft were neglected (src-056). AARO's 2024 information paper (src-058) explains how forced perspective and parallax can inflate apparent size and speed; it does not analyze GoFast.
