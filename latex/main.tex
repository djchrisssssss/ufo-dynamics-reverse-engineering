A Physics-Based Assessment of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena}

\author{Kris Lai}

\date{March 2026}

\begin{abstract}
This paper presents a systematic physics-based assessment of reported UFO/UAP
flight characteristics. Drawing upon publicly available peer-reviewed literature,
declassified government documents, and open-source intelligence, it evaluates
whether known or theoretically plausible physical mechanisms---including
Alcubierre warp metrics, nuclear fusion, the dynamical Casimir effect, and
metamaterials---could account for the ``Five Observables'' taxonomy. The analysis
is grounded in established general relativity, quantum field theory, nuclear
physics, electromagnetic theory, and materials science.
\textbf{Principal finding:} Public UAP data are generally too poorly calibrated to
establish the extreme kinematics and absent signatures used as inputs. General
relativity admits mathematical warp metrics, but no physically realizable,
conserved, stable, and controllable source for the required stress-energy has
been demonstrated. GR already couples stress-energy to curvature; the unresolved
issue is source realization and control, not a separate energy-to-curvature
conversion interaction. Claims of medium isolation, universal THz/DCE emission, or
morphology--velocity sequences are not derived by the cited literature.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Key Judgments}

Confidence levels and estimative language follow Intelligence Community
Directive~203 (Appendix~\ref{app:confidence}). The bracketed tags refer to the
notes that follow the judgments.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item We assess with \textbf{high confidence} that the public UAP record is not
    sufficiently calibrated to establish the extreme trajectories, absent
    signatures, or transmedium performance used as inputs in this report. General
    relativity admits mathematical geometries with unusual coordinate motion, but
    that fact does not establish that the required stress-energy is physically
    realizable or that any UAP followed such a trajectory. [KJ-1] All mechanism
    comparisons in this assessment are therefore conditional and model-dependent.
  \item We assess with \textbf{high confidence} that the Alcubierre warp metric
    (1994) is a valid solution to the Einstein field equations that would, in
    principle, permit apparent superluminal motion without local violation of
    special relativity. [KJ-2] However, we assess with \textbf{high confidence}
    that all superluminal warp drive configurations require exotic matter
    (negative energy density) that has not been produced in macroscopic
    quantities. [KJ-3]
  \item\label{kj:tidal} We assess with \textbf{high confidence} that a chosen
    geodesic worldline has zero proper acceleration. This does \emph{not} imply
    zero tidal loading across a finite vehicle, nor does it validate the
    source-dependent \textit{Nimitz} acceleration estimate. Habitability requires a
    specified dynamical metric and explicit bounds on geodesic deviation
    throughout the occupied volume. [KJ-4]
  \item We assess with \textbf{high confidence} that deuterium--tritium fusion
    releases approximately $3.37\times10^{14}$~J/kg of combined reactants and is
    the most experimentally accessible high-yield fusion reaction. It is not the
    highest-specific-energy known process, and specific energy alone does not
    establish it as a viable power source for metric engineering. [KJ-5] Compact,
    mobile fusion remains undemonstrated and its timeline is uncertain.
  \item We assess with \textbf{high confidence} that the cited dynamical Casimir
    experiment demonstrates microwave photon production in a deliberately
    time-modulated superconducting boundary. We also assess with \textbf{high
    confidence} that this result does not, by itself, predict any electromagnetic
    spectrum for a hypothetical warp metric: no cited calculation establishes
    broadband, terahertz, or soft-X-ray emission from a subluminal warp
    configuration. [KJ-6]
  \item We assess with \textbf{high confidence} that some reported symptoms, such
    as erythema or burns, can be produced by sufficiently intense electromagnetic
    exposure, but we have \textbf{low confidence} in attributing any reported case
    to such exposure, because the symptoms are nonspecific and public cases
    generally lack measured field spectra, dose, clinical documentation, controls,
    and exclusion of alternative causes. Neither electromagnetic exposure nor UAP
    causation is established. [KJ-7]
  \item We assess with \textbf{high confidence}, following the AARO/Oak Ridge
    National Laboratory analysis, that the tested bismuth--magnesium specimen was
    manufactured terrestrially and was highly unlikely to function as a bismuth
    terahertz waveguide. Its chain of custody and original purpose remain
    unresolved; AARO assesses it as likely a test object, manufacturing product or
    byproduct, or component of mid-20th-century magnesium-alloy aerospace studies.
    The official analyses do not identify it as Betterton--Kroll dross. [KJ-8]
  \item We assess with \textbf{high confidence} that no single propulsion framework
    in the current literature explains the full spectrum of reported UAP
    observables simultaneously. The integration of spacetime engineering, compact
    energy sources, and advanced materials into a coherent propulsion system
    remains beyond the horizon of current theoretical physics. [KJ-9]
  \item We assess with \textbf{high confidence} that mathematical validity of a
    warp metric is not evidence of physical realizability. Energy-condition
    violations, quantum inequalities, formation, stability, matter coupling, and
    control are foundational constraints that materials improvements alone do not
    resolve. [KJ-10a]
  \item We assess with \textbf{moderate confidence} that the most productive UAP
    research priority is calibrated, synchronized, multi-sensor collection with
    complete metadata and quantified uncertainties. Fusion, materials,
    quantum-field experiments, and analog gravity remain valuable independent

  \item We assess with \textbf{high confidence} that general relativity already

    equations, and that the unresolved problem is realizing, dynamically forming,
    and controlling the particular stress-energy tensor required by a target
    metric---including its magnitude, sign, anisotropy, gradients, conservation,

\section{Introduction and Historical Context}\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Background and Motivation}\label{sec:background}

This work was prompted by a close reading of \textit{Imminent: Inside the
Pentagon's Hunt for UFOs}~(William Morrow, 2024) by Luis
Elizondo~\cite{Elizondo2024}. The memoir is a secondary, participant account and
is used here as a source of hypotheses rather than as validation of physical
observations. AARO distinguishes the official 2009--2012 AAWSAP contract from a
later informal use of the AATIP label and reports that the exploratory DIRDs were
not thoroughly peer reviewed~\cite{AARO2024}.

Claims such as instantaneous acceleration, transmedium travel, and absence of
sonic booms can be translated into testable physical quantities, but the public
evidence does not establish that every claimed observable occurred as described.
This assessment therefore treats the Five Observables and the DIRDs as prompts for
conditional analysis, separates source-dependent estimates from measurements, and
asks what additional calibrated data would be required before any propulsion
inference is justified.

\subsection{Terminology: UFO, UAP, and Scope of This Document}\label{sec:terminology}

Before proceeding, a note on terminology is necessary. Two terms recur
throughout this document and the broader literature:

\begin{description}
  \item[UFO (Unidentified Flying Object)] The original term, coined in 1953 by
    USAF Captain Edward~J.\ Ruppelt to replace the colloquial ``flying saucer.''
    A UFO refers specifically to an \emph{observed airborne object} whose
    identity cannot be determined by the observer. The term implies a discrete
    physical object in flight.
  \item[UAP (Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena; formerly Unidentified Aerial
    Phenomena)] The broader category used by the U.S.\ Department of Defense and
    the intelligence community. The ``aerial'' form was in DoD use by 2020 (UAP
    Task Force); the FY2023 National Defense Authorization Act (Public
    Law~117-263, December~2022) redefined UAP as unidentified \emph{anomalous}
    phenomena, explicitly covering airborne, submerged, and transmedium objects.
    UAP encompasses not only flying objects~(UFOs) but also airborne sensor
    anomalies, transmedium phenomena, underwater detections, and events that may
    not correspond to a physical object at all (e.g., atmospheric plasma,
    electronic spoofing, optical illusions).
\end{description}

\noindent
In set-theoretic terms: $\text{UFO}\subset\text{UAP}$. Every UFO is a UAP, but
not every UAP is a UFO. A radar return with no visual confirmation, an
unexplained underwater sonar contact, or a satellite infrared anomaly would be
classified as UAP but not necessarily as a UFO.

\paragraph{Convention in this document.}
This report primarily uses \textbf{``UAP''} as the default term, consistent with
current official nomenclature (ODNI, AARO, DoD). However, \textbf{``UFO''} is
used where the context specifically refers to a discrete physical flying
object---particularly in discussing propulsion mechanisms, vehicle geometry, warp
bubble morphology, and materials analysis, where the physics presupposes a
tangible craft. The title retains ``UFO'' for its universal recognizability and
because this assessment focuses primarily on the physics of physical flying
objects, not the full breadth of anomalous phenomena.

\subsection{The UAP Question: From Project Blue Book to AARO}\label{sec:bluebook-to-aaro}

The systematic investigation of unidentified aerial phenomena by the United States
government spans more than seven decades. Project Sign~(1947), Project
Grudge~(1949), and Project Blue Book~(1952--1969) collectively examined over
12\,000 reports. A separate USAF-funded study at the University of Colorado,
directed by physicist Dr.~Edward Condon (released January~1969), concluded that
further extensive study of UFOs was unlikely to advance scientific knowledge; the
Air Force terminated Blue Book in December~1969, effectively ending official
investigation for nearly four decades~\cite{AARO2024}.

The landscape shifted dramatically in December~2017, when investigative reporting
by the \textit{New York Times} revealed the existence of the Advanced Aerospace
Threat Identification Program~(AATIP), which the \textit{Times} described as a
Pentagon initiative that operated from 2007 to 2012 with \$22 million in funding
secured by Senator Harry Reid with support from Senators Ted Stevens and Daniel
Inouye. AARO's 2024 historical review identifies the official program as the
DIA-managed Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program~(AAWSAP,
2009--2012), funded by \$22 million appropriated in fiscal years 2008 and 2010
and contracted to Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies~(BAASS); it states that
the AATIP name was used interchangeably but that AATIP ``was never an official DoD
program''~\cite{AARO2024}.

In June~2021, the Office of the Director of National Intelligence~(ODNI)
published its \textit{Preliminary Assessment: Unidentified Aerial Phenomena},
cataloguing 144 UAP reports from November~2004 to March~2021~\cite{ODNI2021}.
The 2022 annual report (released January~2023) catalogued 510 reports in total:
the 144 from the preliminary assessment plus 366 newly identified reports. Of the
366, an initial characterization attributed 195 to balloon-like objects~(163),
UAS-like objects~(26), or clutter~(6), leaving 171 uncharacterized and
unattributed; ODNI stated that \emph{some} of these appeared to demonstrate
unusual flight characteristics or performance capabilities~\cite{ODNI2023}.

In July~2022, the Department of Defense established the All-domain Anomaly
Resolution Office~(AARO) to provide a permanent institutional home for UAP
investigation. AARO's Historical Record Report, Volume~1 (March~2024), reviewed
the entire history of U.S.\ government UAP investigation and stated that AARO
``found no evidence that any USG investigation, academic-sponsored research, or
official review panel has confirmed that any sighting of a UAP represented
extraterrestrial technology,'' and ``no empirical evidence for claims that the USG
and private companies have been reverse-engineering extraterrestrial
technology''~\cite{AARO2024}.

This report does not take a position on the origin of UAP. Instead, it asks a
narrower question: \textbf{what does established physics tell us about the
feasibility of the reported flight characteristics?}

\subsection{The AAWSAP/AATIP Program and the 38 DIRDs}\label{sec:dirds}

Under the DIA-managed AAWSAP contract, the contractor (BAASS) delivered 38 Defense
Intelligence Reference Document~(DIRD) entries to the DIA; the released copies are
dated between December~2009 and January~2011. These papers were authored by
recognized experts---including Dr.~Harold Puthoff (EarthTech International),
Dr.~Eric Davis (EarthTech International), Dr.~Friedwardt Winterberg (University of
Nevada, Reno), and Dr.~Ulf Leonhardt (University of St.~Andrews)---and surveyed
the frontiers of physics and engineering relevant to advanced aerospace
propulsion~\cite{AARO2024,DIRD_ObousyDavis,DIRD_Puthoff,DIRD_Davis_Wormholes,DIRD_Winterberg,DIRD_Leonhardt,DIRD_Davis_Antigravity}.

The DIRDs covered a remarkable breadth of topics:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Spacetime physics:} warp drives, traversable wormholes, negative
        energy, dark energy manipulation, high-frequency gravitational waves.
  \item \textbf{Propulsion:} vacuum engineering, positron propulsion, laser
        lightcraft, MHD air-breathing propulsion, negative mass propulsion.
  \item \textbf{Energy:} inertial electrostatic confinement fusion, advanced
        nuclear propulsion, aneutronic fusion, quantum vacuum energy extraction,
        ultracapacitors.
  \item \textbf{Materials:} metallic glasses, metamaterials, programmable matter,
        biomaterials, metallic spintronics.
  \item \textbf{Detection and effects:} hypersonic vehicle tracking, invisibility
        cloaking, biological field effects.
  \item \textbf{Other:} brain--machine interfaces, quantum computing, quantum
        entanglement communications, the Drake equation.
\end{itemize}

DIA released 37 DIRDs (1\,473 pages) under FOIA on March~25, 2022, in response to
a 2018 request by The Black Vault; the documents are marked UNCLASSIFIED//FOR
OFFICIAL USE ONLY and were withheld only in part (e.g., author names) under FOIA
exemptions (b)(3) and (b)(6). The 38th entry on the DIA list is a SECRET/NOFORN
version of the high-energy laser weapons DIRD, whose unclassified version (\#23)
was released.

These documents are used here as a survey of frontier and speculative aerospace
topics commissioned under AAWSAP. AARO reports that UAP investigation was not
specifically part of the contract's statement of work and that these exploratory
papers were never thoroughly peer reviewed; they are therefore not treated as an
official mapping of UAP observables onto physics~\cite{AARO2024}.

\subsection{Scope and Methodology}\label{sec:scope}

This report adopts the following methodology:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Identify reported observables} from official government sources
        (ODNI and AARO reports) and from participant accounts (e.g., the Five
        Observables as popularized by Elizondo), keeping the two categories
        distinct.
  \item \textbf{Map each observable} onto the relevant domain of physics.
  \item \textbf{Evaluate feasibility} using the verified $\to$ theoretical
        $\to$ speculative hierarchy.
  \item \textbf{Quantify constraints} using key equations and order-of-magnitude
        estimates.
  \item \textbf{Identify gaps} where current physics provides no satisfactory
        explanation.
  \item \textbf{Recommend research directions} that could resolve key unknowns.
\end{enumerate}

The goal is not to prove or disprove any specific claim about UAP, but to provide
a rigorous scientific framework for evaluating them. Each claim is evaluated
against the hierarchy of scientific evidence and annotated, where relevant, with
the verification markers defined in Appendix~\ref{app:confidence}
(\textsc{Verified}, \textsc{Theoretical}, \textsc{Speculative}, and
\textsc{Assessed}). Origin hypotheses (extraterrestrial, interdimensional,
adversarial technology, etc.), witness credibility assessment or sociological
analysis, and classified sensor data or unreleased government analyses are outside
the scope of this assessment.

\textbf{Evidence hierarchy and DIRD weighting.} This assessment draws on sources
of varying evidential authority. Peer-reviewed articles published in recognized
journals (e.g., \textit{Physical Review}, \textit{Nature}, \textit{Classical and
Quantum Gravity}) carry the highest evidential weight. Government documents from
ODNI, AARO, and DoD are treated as authoritative primary sources for observational
claims and policy context. The 37 FOIA-released Defense Intelligence Reference
Documents~(DIRDs), while authored by recognized physicists and delivered to the
DIA under the AAWSAP contract, were never thoroughly peer reviewed (AARO,
2024)~\cite{AARO2024}; they are treated as Tier~2 expert technical assessments
whose theoretical claims are cross-checked against peer-reviewed literature
wherever possible. Preprints carry the lowest evidential weight.

\section{Observable Characteristics --- A Physics Framework}\label{sec:observables}

\subsection{Five Observables Taxonomy}\label{sec:five-observables}

Luis Elizondo---who states that he directed AATIP, a role the Department of
Defense has publicly disputed and that is difficult to reconcile with AARO's
description of post-2010 ``AATIP'' as an informal effort without dedicated
personnel or budget---popularized five categories of reported UAP behavior that
conventional aerospace technology cannot readily explain. These ``Five
Observables'' are widely cited but are not a DoD, ODNI, or AARO classification
scheme~\cite{Elizondo2024}:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Instantaneous acceleration}---sudden changes in velocity without
        apparent transitional acceleration.
  \item \textbf{Hypersonic velocity without signatures}---speeds exceeding
        Mach~5 with no sonic boom, exhaust plume, or thermal signature.
  \item \textbf{Low observability}---reduced radar cross-section and/or visual
        detectability despite confirmed presence.
  \item \textbf{Anti-gravity lift}---sustained flight without visible wings,
        rotors, exhaust, or other conventional lift mechanisms.
  \item \textbf{Transmedium travel}---seamless transition between air, water,
        and potentially space without performance degradation.
\end{enumerate}

To these, subsequent analyses have added: (6)~biological effects---physiological
symptoms reported by individuals in proximity to UAP; and
(7)~electromagnetic interference---disruption of electronic systems and
communications near UAP. This section examines each observable through the lens
of established physics.

\subsection{Observational Data Quality Assessment}\label{sec:data-quality}

Before proceeding to the physics analysis of each observable, a critical assessment
of the underlying observational data is necessary. This assessment uses
sensor-derived estimates as inputs; the physics analysis that follows is only as
strong as those inputs.

\textbf{Data provenance framework.} The core UAP cases cited in this report vary
significantly in data quality; we grade key cases by sensor corroboration,
calibration status, and chain of custody. The 2004 USS~\textit{Nimitz} encounter
receives grade~C (source-dependent, uncalibrated): its primary data are witness
accounts of SPY-1 tracks, an ATFLIR infrared clip, and visual reports. Multiple
modalities were reported, but the specific rapid-descent estimate was not
independently reproduced across public sensors; the radar was operational-grade,
detailed track data are not public, and ATFLIR is not calibrated for
photogrammetry; and the acceleration estimate relies on witness-reported track
parameters rather than released detailed radar data. The Gimbal~(2015) ATFLIR
video receives grade~C (single sensor, limited context): no radar data were
released, the targeting pod is not calibrated for scientific measurement, and only
a short clip---not the full encounter data---has been released. The GoFast~(2015)
ATFLIR video receives grade~C$-$ (single sensor, likely resolved): the NASA UAP
Independent Study Team's analysis is consistent with an object at
${\sim}\SI{13000}{ft}$ moving at ${\sim}\SI{40}{mph}$ (wind-like), with aircraft
wind neglected~\cite{NASA_UAP2023}.

\textbf{Model sensitivity of the $5{,}370\,g$ scenario.} Knuth, Powell, and
Reali~(2019) modeled a SPY-1 descent reported by Senior Chief Kevin Day; they did
not have the detailed radar track needed to independently reconstruct the
motion~\cite{Knuth2019}. For a witness-reported descent of $8{,}530\pm90$~m in
$0.78\pm0.08$~s, with the object assumed to start and end approximately at rest,
their Monte Carlo model yielded a modal acceleration of
$5{,}370\,(+1{,}430/{-}820)\,g$. The ${-}820\,g$ term is the lower uncertainty on
that modal estimate, not a measured lower endpoint. The result depends on an
assumed accelerate-then-decelerate profile and scales as $a=4d/t^{2}$
(Table~\ref{tab:nimitz-sensitivity}).

\textbf{Conditional framing.} This assessment adopts a conditional analytical
posture: \emph{``if the reported observations are accurate within reasonable
bounds, what physics would be required?''} The physics analysis below is
conditional on the observational inputs. It does not independently verify the raw
sensor data (which remains classified) or the operator interpretations. Where data
quality is low, the corresponding physics analysis inherits that uncertainty and
is marked accordingly.

\subsection{Instantaneous Acceleration}\label{sec:acceleration}

The most dramatic claimed observable is extreme acceleration. For the 2004
USS~\textit{Nimitz} encounter, Knuth, Powell, and Reali used witness-reported
parameters for a descent from approximately \SI{28000}{ft} to \SI{50}{ft} in
$0.78\pm0.08$~s and obtained a model-dependent modal estimate of
$5{,}370\,(+1{,}430/{-}820)\,g$. Detailed radar tracks are not public, so the
value is a source-dependent scenario estimate rather than a measurement (see
Sec.~\ref{sec:data-quality})~\cite{Knuth2019}.

\textbf{Biological constraints.} The human body's tolerance for sustained
acceleration is approximately $9\,g$ with anti-$g$ equipment. Brief tolerance
extends to ${\sim}20\,g$ for trained individuals, and survivable crash impacts
have been recorded at ${\sim}100$--$215\,g$ for fractions of a second. An
acceleration of $5{,}370\,g$ sustained for even a fraction of a second would be
instantly fatal to any known biological organism.

\textbf{Structural constraints.} Rocket-boosted interceptors have demonstrated
accelerations of a few hundred $g$ (${\approx}400\,g$ reported for the HIBEX
booster). Gun-launched projectiles, including their hardened fuzes and guidance
electronics, survive $>10{,}000\,g$, but only for milliseconds during launch;
laboratory ultracentrifuges sustain ${\sim}10^{6}\,g$ only for small,
purpose-built rotors. Neither corresponds to sustained maneuvering of a complex
vehicle.

\textbf{Proper acceleration and tidal loading.}\ Einstein's equivalence
principle is verified to ${\sim}10^{-15}$ precision by the MICROSCOPE
satellite~\cite{Touboul2022}. A freely falling point particle follows a geodesic
and its accelerometer reads zero proper acceleration; this is not a contrapositive
inference and does not by itself describe a finite craft~\cite{MTW1973,Wald1984}.

If a specified metric placed a chosen reference worldline on a geodesic, that
worldline could have zero proper acceleration while displaying coordinate
acceleration. Occupants separated across a finite cabin can nevertheless
experience relative acceleration from curvature. The reference-worldline equation
is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geodesic}
  \frac{d^{2}x^{\mu}}{d\tau^{2}}
  + \Gamma^{\mu}{}_{\alpha\beta}\,
    \frac{dx^{\alpha}}{d\tau}\,\frac{dx^{\beta}}{d\tau} = 0\,,
\end{equation}
where $x^{\mu}$ are spacetime coordinates, $\tau$ is proper time, and
$\Gamma^{\mu}{}_{\alpha\beta}$ are the Christoffel symbols of the metric
(coordinate-dependent connection coefficients; curvature itself is encoded in the
Riemann tensor)~\cite{MTW1973,Wald1984}. For a finite craft, survivability
additionally requires bounding geodesic deviation,
$D^{2}\xi^{\hat{i}}/d\tau^{2}=-c^{2}R^{\hat{i}}{}_{\hat{0}\hat{j}\hat{0}}\,\xi^{\hat{j}}$,
throughout the occupied volume.

This is not speculative physics---it is the same principle that keeps astronauts
in weightless free fall aboard the International Space Station, despite orbiting
Earth at ${\sim}\SI{7.7}{km/s}$ with a centripetal acceleration of
${\sim}\SI{8.7}{m/s^{2}}$. The astronauts follow a geodesic and feel no
force~\cite{MTW1973,Wald1984}.

\textbf{Assessment:} Zero proper acceleration on a selected geodesic is an
established local statement, but its application to UAP is conditional on
unverified kinematics and a fully specified metric. It does not eliminate tidal
loading, matter interaction, bubble formation, or source constraints. General
relativity therefore supplies a modeling possibility, not an explanation of the
\textit{Nimitz} claim~\cite{Touboul2022,MTW1973,Wald1984,Knuth2019}.

\subsection{Hypersonic Velocity Without Signatures}\label{sec:hypersonic}

Conventional objects moving through Earth's atmosphere at hypersonic speeds
($>\text{Mach}\;5$) produce several inevitable signatures:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Sonic boom:} a shock wave cone forms when an object exceeds the
        local speed of sound (${\sim}\SI{343}{m/s}$ in air at \SI{20}{\celsius}). This is a
        fundamental consequence of wave mechanics and cannot be avoided by
        aerodynamic design alone.
  \item \textbf{Thermal signature:} aerodynamic heating at hypersonic speeds
        raises surface temperatures to thousands of kelvin. The Space Shuttle's
        thermal protection system experienced ${\sim}\SI{1650}{\degreeCelsius}$
        during reentry at approximately Mach~25.
  \item \textbf{Exhaust plume:} conventional propulsion (turbojets, ramjets,
        scramjets, rockets) produces visible and infrared-detectable exhaust.
\end{itemize}
Multiple UAP reports describe objects traveling at hypersonic speeds without any
of these signatures.

\textbf{Conventional explanations.} Plasma sheath formation at hypersonic speeds
can partially absorb radar signals and modify the electromagnetic signature of a
vehicle---a well-studied phenomenon in re-entry vehicle design. However, plasma
formation does not eliminate sonic booms or thermal signatures; if anything, it
intensifies them.

\textbf{Matter coupling in a warp metric.} Being locally at rest in a coordinate
construction does not make a vehicle impermeable to surrounding matter. The
Alcubierre metric alone supplies no material boundary and does not establish that
air is excluded from the bubble. Particle trajectories and interactions must be
solved for the chosen metric and quantum/classical state~\cite{Alcubierre1994}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item published test-particle calculations allow external matter to enter or
        accumulate near parts of a warp geometry;
  \item particle energies and directions can be shifted by the geometry;
  \item shock formation, heating, drag, and wake therefore require an explicit
        matter-coupling calculation.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Assessment:} A confirmed material object moving hypersonically through air
without shock or heating would require explanation, but the public reports do not
yet establish all of those conditions simultaneously. A generic warp metric does
not, by itself, predict isolation from air and therefore cannot currently explain
the claimed absence of signatures~\cite{Alcubierre1994}.

\subsection{Low Observability}\label{sec:low-observability}

Low observability---reduced detectability by radar and/or visual means---is the
most readily explicable of the Five Observables, as significant conventional
technology exists in this domain.

\textbf{Radar cross-section reduction} is a mature technology. The F-117 Nighthawk
(operational 1983--2008) achieved radar cross-sections reportedly on the order of
$0.001$--$\SI{0.01}{m^{2}}$, compared to ${\sim}5$--$\SI{100}{m^{2}}$ for
conventional fighter aircraft. Modern stealth technology employs geometric
shaping, radar-absorbing materials~(RAM), and active cancellation systems.

\textbf{Metamaterial cloaking.} Pendry, Schurig, and Smith~(2006) provided the
theoretical foundation for electromagnetic cloaking via transformation
optics---using metamaterials to guide electromagnetic waves around an
object~\cite{PendrySchurig2006}. Experimental demonstrations have been achieved at
microwave frequencies but remain impractical at optical wavelengths due to
bandwidth limitations, absorption losses, and fabrication constraints.

\textbf{Gravitational lensing as concealment.} In principle, if a vehicle distorts
spacetime sufficiently, light rays passing near it would be deflected,
potentially rendering the vehicle partially or wholly invisible from certain
viewing angles. However, the energy requirements for producing detectable
gravitational lensing at human-accessible scales are extraordinarily high (see
Sec.~\ref{sec:lensing})~\cite{DysonEddington1920,MTW1973}.

\textbf{Assessment:} Low observability is \emph{achievable} with conventional
technology and does not require exotic physics. Metamaterial-based invisibility is
\emph{theoretically sound} but technologically immature. Gravitational lensing as
a concealment mechanism is \emph{speculative} and energetically implausible.

\subsection{Gravitational Lensing Effect}\label{sec:lensing}

Several UAP reports describe visual distortions around the object---a ``shimmer''
or ``haze'' suggestive of light refraction or bending. Some analysts have
attributed this to localized gravitational lensing.

\textbf{Established physics of gravitational lensing.} General relativity predicts
that mass-energy curves spacetime, and light follows geodesics through this curved
geometry. The deflection angle for a light ray passing a mass $M$ at impact
parameter $b$ is~\cite{MTW1973,Wald1984}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:light-deflection}
  \theta = \frac{4GM}{c^{2}\,b}\,.
\end{equation}
This was first confirmed during the total solar eclipse of May~29, 1919, by Dyson,
Eddington, and Davidson, whose Sobral and Pr\'{\i}ncipe plates gave deflections of
about \SI{1.98}{arcsec} and \SI{1.61}{arcsec} for starlight at the solar
limb---consistent with Einstein's \SI{1.75}{arcsec} prediction and not with the
Newtonian \SI{0.87}{arcsec}~\cite{DysonEddington1920,GilmoreTauschPebody2022}.
Gravitational lensing is now a cornerstone of observational cosmology, used to map
dark matter distributions and detect exoplanets via
microlensing~\cite{DysonEddington1920}.

\textbf{Energy scale problem.} A point-mass estimate illustrates the scale without
implying that a warp stress tensor is point-like. For a \SI{1}{arcsec}
deflection, Eq.~\eqref{eq:light-deflection} gives $M\approx1.6\times10^{22}$~kg at
$b=\SI{10}{m}$ and $M\approx1.6\times10^{24}$~kg at $b=\SI{1}{km}$,
corresponding to $1.5\times10^{39}$--$1.5\times10^{41}$~J. These are dwarf-planet
to planetary mass-equivalents, not compact stellar masses, but a local source would
still produce catastrophic gravity and tides. An extended warp geometry requires
full null-geodesic ray tracing rather than this point-mass
scaling~\cite{DysonEddington1920,MTW1973}. An object generating such curvature
would also produce overwhelming tidal forces on its surroundings---effects that
are conspicuously absent from UAP reports.

\textbf{Alternative explanations.} Visual distortions around UAP could result from
heated air (thermal mirage effects) from high-energy systems, plasma formation in
the surrounding atmosphere, refractive index gradients from intense
electromagnetic fields, or optical artifacts from sensor systems. Each of these
explanations involves well-understood physics and does not require gravitational
effects~\cite{Condign2000,AARO_Parallax2024}.

\textbf{Assessment:} While gravitational lensing is \emph{verified} astrophysics,
its application to UAP-scale phenomena is \emph{energetically implausible} by many
orders of magnitude. The observed visual distortions are more \emph{likely}
attributable to thermal, plasma, or electromagnetic effects.

\subsection{Transmedium Travel}\label{sec:transmedium}

Perhaps the most challenging observable from a conventional physics perspective is
the reported ability to transition seamlessly between air and water without
performance degradation.

\textbf{The density barrier.} Water is approximately 830 times denser than air at
sea level. An object entering water from air at high speed encounters an enormous
dynamic pressure discontinuity:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dynamic-pressure}
  q = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^{2}\,.
\end{equation}
At \SI{100}{m/s}, the dynamic pressure in air is ${\sim}\SI{6}{kPa}$; in water,
it is ${\sim}\SI{5}{MPa}$---approximately 830 times greater, matching the density
ratio (since $q \propto \rho$ at fixed velocity). This represents an effectively
instantaneous structural load that would destroy most aerospace vehicles.

\textbf{Supercavitation.} Supercavitation---creating a gas cavity envelope around
an underwater vehicle---reduces hydrodynamic drag by approximately 90\%. The
Russian VA-111 Shkval torpedo uses this principle to achieve speeds of
${\sim}200$ knots underwater. However, supercavitation requires deliberate
cavitator design, does not eliminate drag entirely, and does not address the
transition dynamics at the air--water boundary.

\textbf{Matter coupling across media.} A warp coordinate construction does not
decouple a vehicle from air or water and supplies no impermeable interface.
Whether matter enters, accumulates, shocks, or transfers momentum depends on a
specified metric, vehicle boundary, and interaction model~\cite{Alcubierre1994}.
Consequently, it does not by itself predict zero deceleration at the air--water
boundary, the absence of cavitation or wake, or the absence of hydrodynamic noise
or heating.

\textbf{Assessment:} If seamless transmedium travel without performance
degradation were established by calibrated above- and below-surface sensors, it
would strongly constrain conventional vehicles. Current public data do not
establish that trajectory, and ``spacetime isolation'' is an additional
unsupported assumption rather than a result of the Alcubierre
metric~\cite{Alcubierre1994}.

\subsection{Alternative and Competing Explanatory Frameworks}\label{sec:alternatives}

Before adopting any single analytical framework, it is necessary to evaluate the
principal competing hypotheses that have been proposed to explain UAP
observations. Each hypothesis is assessed for its ability to account for the full
Five Observables taxonomy.

\textbf{1.\ Atmospheric plasma and ball lightning.} The UK Ministry of Defence's
Project Condign study (2000, declassified 2006) concluded that many UAP sightings
are attributable to buoyant charged plasma formations in the atmosphere---a
natural phenomenon capable of producing luminous, rapidly moving aerial
phenomena~\cite{Condign2000}. Plasma can account for luminosity, apparent rapid
motion, electromagnetic interference, and some biological effects
(microwave-frequency heating). However, plasma formations cannot explain solid
radar returns from structured objects, transmedium travel (air-to-water
transition), geometric consistency across multiple viewing angles, or sustained
hypersonic velocity. The plasma hypothesis is a valid partial explanation for a
subset of UAP but cannot unify all Five Observables.

\textbf{2.\ Sensor artifacts and misinterpretation.} The NASA UAP Independent
Study Team (2023) used the on-screen range, elevation angle, and aircraft altitude
in the ``GoFast'' video---one of the three canonical Pentagon UAP videos---to
estimate that the object was at about \SI{13000}{ft} and moving at roughly
\SI{40}{mph}, consistent with drifting in the wind, with the impression of high
speed due largely to the fast-moving sensor and parallax; the panel noted residual
uncertainty because wind effects on the aircraft were
neglected~\cite{NASA_UAP2023}. AARO's 2024 information paper on forced perspective
and parallax explains how these effects can produce apparently excessive sizes and
speeds in UAP reports~\cite{AARO_Parallax2024}. Independent analysts have shown
that FLIR video artifacts (gimbal derotation, infrared glare, parallax) can create
the appearance of extraordinary maneuvers from mundane objects. This is a
critically important filter: many individual UAP cases may be resolvable through
careful sensor analysis alone. However, cases reported to involve several
modalities---such as the 2004 USS~\textit{Nimitz} encounter (witness-reported
SPY-1 tracks, visual reports, and an ATFLIR clip recorded on a later
sortie)---would be more resistant to single-sensor-artifact explanations if the
underlying multi-sensor data were released; in the public record they are not
(Sec.~\ref{sec:data-quality}). The NASA UAP Independent Study Team (2023)
recommended improved sensor calibration and data collection standards to reduce
this ambiguity~\cite{NASA_UAP2023}.

\textbf{3.\ Adversarial technology.} Foreign aerospace technology---advanced
drones, hypersonic vehicles, or novel electronic warfare systems---is a plausible
explanation for some fraction of UAP, particularly those observed near military
installations or training areas. However, the reported flight characteristics
(accelerations of order $10^{3}\,g$, transmedium travel, absence of exhaust or
propulsive signatures), if accurate, would exceed any demonstrated flight-vehicle
capability (cf.\ ${\sim}400\,g$ for the HIBEX booster). If the observations are
accurate and the objects are adversarial technology, the technology itself
requires the same physics analysis this paper provides---the question shifts from
``what are they?'' to ``what physics enables them?''

\textbf{4.\ Cognitive and perceptual bias.} Observer expectation effects,
pareidolia, attention-driven motion illusions, and memory distortion are
well-documented psychological phenomena that can produce or amplify anomalous
perceptions. These factors may explain a significant fraction of witness reports
that lack instrumental corroboration. They do not, however, explain calibrated
radar returns, infrared signatures recorded on targeting systems, or observations
confirmed by multiple independent sensor systems. Cognitive bias is a valid concern
for single-witness visual reports but diminishes in explanatory power as the
number of independent, calibrated sensors increases.

\textbf{Framework selection rationale.} This assessment adopts the warp bubble
(spacetime distortion) framework as its primary conditional lens. That is not
because it is the most probable explanation for UAP, nor because it has been shown
to explain the Five Observables: Secs.~\ref{sec:acceleration},
\ref{sec:hypersonic}, \ref{sec:transmedium}, and~\ref{sec:warp-bubble} show that a
generic warp metric does not by itself predict tidal-load-free acceleration, the
absence of shocks and heating, or seamless transmedium travel without explicit
matter-coupling, radiation, and ray-tracing calculations, and it requires
stress-energy that has not been shown to be physically realizable. It is used
because it is a frequently invoked single-mechanism proposal (e.g., DIRDs~\#13
and~\#15~\cite{DIRD_ObousyDavis,DIRD_Puthoff}) and provides a well-defined
general-relativistic setting. The plasma, sensor artifact, adversarial technology,
and cognitive bias hypotheses each explain subsets of the observables, and the
warp framework is not more parsimonious than they are, because it does not yet
explain any observable quantitatively. It is therefore used here as a ``what would
it take?'' exercise in physics, not as a claim about what UAP actually are. The
alternative frameworks discussed above should be considered active competitors;
future calibrated sensor data (Sec.~\ref{sec:research}) may favor one or more of
these alternatives over the spacetime distortion
hypothesis~\cite{Alcubierre1994,BirrellDavies1982,NASA_UAP2023,Condign2000,AARO_Parallax2024}.

\section{Propulsion Mechanisms --- Spacetime Engineering}\label{sec:propulsion}

\subsection{GR Foundations}\label{sec:gr-foundations}

The mathematical language of UAP propulsion analysis is general
relativity~(GR). Einstein's field equations relate the geometry of spacetime
(left side) to the distribution of matter and energy (right
side)~\cite{MTW1973,Wald1984}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:einstein}
  G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda\,g_{\mu\nu}
  = \frac{8\pi G}{c^{4}}\,T_{\mu\nu}\,,
\end{equation}
where $G_{\mu\nu}$ is the Einstein tensor (encoding spacetime curvature),
$\Lambda$ is the cosmological constant, $g_{\mu\nu}$ is the metric tensor, $G$ is
Newton's gravitational constant, $c$ is the speed of light, and $T_{\mu\nu}$ is
the stress-energy tensor (encoding matter and energy distribution).

A key feature of GR is that it can be solved ``in reverse'': one can specify a
desired spacetime geometry and then determine what matter-energy distribution
would be required to produce it. This approach---sometimes called ``metric
engineering'' or ``spacetime engineering''---is the foundation of warp drive
research~\cite{DIRD_Puthoff,MTW1973,Wald1984}.

\subsection{Alcubierre Warp Drive}\label{sec:alcubierre}

In 1994, Mexican physicist Miguel Alcubierre published a landmark paper
demonstrating that general relativity admits a spacetime metric permitting
apparent faster-than-light travel~\cite{Alcubierre1994}. The Alcubierre metric
takes the form:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alcubierre}
  ds^{2} = -c^{2}\,dt^{2}
  + \bigl(dx - v_{s}(t)\,f(r_{s})\,dt\bigr)^{2}
  + dy^{2} + dz^{2}\,,
\end{equation}
where $v_{s}(t)$ is the velocity of the warp bubble's center, $r_{s}$ is the
distance from the bubble center, and $f(r_{s})$ is a ``top-hat'' shaping function
satisfying $f(0)=1$ and $f(r\to\infty)=0$.

\textbf{Physical interpretation:} Space contracts ahead of the bubble and expands
behind it. The vehicle inside the bubble rides a ``wave'' of spacetime, analogous
to a surfer on an ocean wave. Crucially:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the vehicle is at rest relative to the local Eulerian (normal) observers
        inside the bubble, even though its coordinate velocity is $v_{s}$;
  \item no local speed limit is violated---each patch of spacetime obeys special
        relativity;
  \item the effective speed is determined by how fast spacetime itself is being
        deformed;
  \item in this idealized metric (unit lapse), observers at rest in the flat
        interior ($f\approx1$) follow geodesics and experience zero proper
        acceleration; finite-body tidal loads and non-ideal profiles must be
        evaluated separately (Secs.~\ref{sec:acceleration}
        and~\ref{sec:tidal-em});
  \item these statements describe a prescribed metric, not a control mechanism.
        For $v_{s}>c$ an observer at the bubble centre is causally disconnected
        from the outer part of the bubble wall, so the bubble can be neither
        created on demand nor steered from inside (Everett and Roman
        1997)~\cite{EverettRoman1997}. The configuration develops horizons
        (Clark, Hiscock, and Larson 1999)~\cite{ClarkHiscockLarson1999}, and
        semiclassical calculations find the renormalized stress-energy growing
        without bound at the front wall, a quantum instability (Hiscock 1997;
        Finazzi, Liberati, and Barcel\'o 2009)~\cite{Hiscock1997,Finazzi2009}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The exotic matter problem.} Alcubierre's original analysis already noted
that the required energy density violates the weak energy condition~(WEC)---meaning
it requires ``exotic matter'' with negative energy density. Subsequent analyses
have quantified this constraint:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Pfenning and Ford (1997)} applied the Ford--Roman quantum
        inequality to Alcubierre's $\tanh$ profile. With their sampling-time
        choice, the wall thickness must satisfy
        $\Delta \lesssim 10^{2}\,v_{b}\,L_{\mathrm{P}}$ ($v_{b}$ in units of $c$),
        i.e., about a hundred to a few hundred Planck lengths (order
        $10^{-33}$~m) for $v_{b}\sim1$. This is not an exact fabrication
        specification. For a \SI{100}{m}-radius bubble the integrated negative
        energy is then $E/c^{2} \lesssim -6.2\times10^{62}\,v_{b}$~kg (a negative
        mass-equivalent of at least this magnitude),
        which they note is roughly ten orders of magnitude more than the mass of
        the visible universe. The result depends strongly on the chosen profile,
        sampling time, and semiclassical
        assumptions~\cite{PfenningFord1997}.
  \item \textbf{Van~Den Broeck (1999)} reduced the integrated negative energy in
        his illustrative construction to a few solar masses by modifying the
        internal geometry, while retaining extreme wall/source
        constraints~\cite{VanDenBroeck1999}.
  \item \textbf{Lentz (2021)} proposed superluminal soliton solutions sourced
        entirely by positive energy (conducting plasma and electromagnetic
        fields)~\cite{Lentz2021}. However, \textbf{Santiago, Schuster, and Visser
        (2022)} demonstrated that Lentz's analysis only checked energy conditions
        for co-moving observers, not all timelike observers as required by the
        WEC. Their analysis showed negative energy density regions
        persist~\cite{SantiagoVisser2022}.
  \item \textbf{Bobrick and Martire (2021)} proposed a general framework in which
        any warp drive is a shell of regular or exotic material moving
        inertially, and argued that subluminal, spherically symmetric
        configurations could in principle be sourced by positive energy. Their
        positive-energy argument evaluates only the Eulerian energy density, and
        Santiago, Schuster, and Visser (2022) dispute their specific spherically
        symmetric example. Bobrick and Martire also argue that every warp drive,
        positive-energy or not, must be accelerated by conventional momentum
        exchange: the shell moves inertially and supplies no propulsion of its
        own~\cite{BobrickMartire2021,SantiagoVisser2022}.
  \item \textbf{Fell and Heisenberg (2021)} claimed superluminal solitonic
        configurations with positive semi-definite Eulerian energy density and
        total energy about four orders of magnitude below a solar mass. Like
        Lentz's construction, the claim examines only Eulerian observers and lies
        in the Nat\'ario class, so the Santiago--Schuster--Visser analysis implies
        weak-energy-condition violation for other
        observers~\cite{FellHeisenberg2021,SantiagoVisser2022}.
  \item \textbf{Fuchs et al.\ (2024)} numerically constructed the first
        constant-velocity, subluminal warp solution reported to satisfy the null,
        weak, strong, and dominant energy conditions: a positive-ADM-mass matter
        shell (2.365 Jupiter masses between radii of \SI{10}{m} and \SI{20}{m})
        with an interior shift of $0.02c$. Its non-unit lapse and non-flat
        spatial metric place it outside the Nat\'ario class covered by the
        Santiago--Schuster--Visser theorem. The authors identify acceleration
        without energy-condition violation as unsolved~\cite{Fuchs2024}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Current consensus:} As of 2026, all known superluminal warp drive
configurations require exotic matter. Positive-energy warp solutions that satisfy
the point-wise energy conditions are known only for constant subluminal velocity
(Fuchs et al.\ 2024). Like any massive shell, they move inertially, must still be
accelerated by conventional momentum exchange, and no
energy-condition-satisfying accelerating solution has been published. The field
remains active, with ongoing research into energy condition relaxations in
modified gravity
theories~\cite{Lentz2021,SantiagoVisser2022,BobrickMartire2021,FellHeisenberg2021,Fuchs2024}.

can geodesics, fields, loads, spectra, and images be

\subsubsection{Apparent Morphology Requires Ray Tracing}\label{sec:morphology}

Apparent shape cannot be inferred from bubble speed or a scalar ``intensity''
alone. It requires integrating null geodesics through a fully specified metric for
a defined emitter, spectrum, vehicle surface, observer position, and observation
time. Published Alcubierre ray-tracing studies (Clark, Hiscock, and Larson 1999;
M\"uller and Weiskopf 2012) find observer- and direction-dependent aberration,
redshift/blueshift, and horizon features for particular metrics; they do not
derive a universal disc $\to$ sphere $\to$ ovoid $\to$ cigar
sequence~\cite{ClarkHiscockLarson1999,MullerWeiskopf2012}.

A Lorentz-factor-style morphology factor such as
$\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}=(1-v_{s}^{2}f^{2}/c^{2})^{-1/2}$ is not an image-formation
solution, and applying the point-mass lens equation to an anisotropic warp stress
tensor is not justified. In general relativity, light that couples only to the
metric follows null geodesics that are achromatic in the geometric-optics limit.
This holds inside the bubble wall's stress-energy as well as in vacuum.
Wavelength-dependent fringing would instead indicate a dispersive medium such as
plasma, a material optical element, or wave-optics effects.

\textbf{Assessment:} No morphology--velocity matrix or chromatic signature is
established. A defensible prediction requires numerical ray tracing from an
explicit metric and uncertainty-aware comparison with calibrated, multi-angle
imagery. Until such a calculation exists, reported shape diversity neither
supports nor falsifies a warp
hypothesis~\cite{Alcubierre1994,MTW1973,Wald1984,DIRD_Puthoff}.

\subsection{Negative Energy and the Quantum Vacuum}\label{sec:negative-energy}

The central obstacle to practical spacetime engineering is the exotic matter
requirement. What does physics say about negative energy?

\textbf{The Casimir effect} demonstrates that quantum vacuum fluctuations are real
and can produce measurable forces---including configurations with negative energy
density between closely spaced conducting plates~\cite{Casimir1948}. First
predicted by Casimir~(1948) and experimentally confirmed by Lamoreaux~(1997), the
effect arises because the boundary conditions imposed by the plates restrict the
modes of virtual photons between them, creating a lower energy density than the
surrounding vacuum~\cite{Lamoreaux1997}.

For ideal, perfectly conducting parallel plates, the renormalized energy density
is $u=-\pi^{2}\hbar c/(720a^{4})$. At $a=\SI{100}{nm}$ this gives approximately
$-\SI{4.33}{J/m^{3}}$. Real materials require
finite-conductivity, temperature, and geometry corrections. No universal warp
target density can be quoted without a matched metric and wall
profile~\cite{Alcubierre1994,PfenningFord1997,Casimir1948,Lamoreaux1997}.

\textbf{Quantum vacuum energy extraction} was explored in DIRD~\#24 (Davis,
``Concepts for Extracting Energy from the Quantum
Vacuum'')~\cite{DIRD_Davis_Vacuum}. While the quantum vacuum contains enormous
theoretical energy density, extracting usable work from it faces fundamental
thermodynamic constraints---the vacuum is, by definition, the lowest energy state,
and one cannot extract energy from a system already in its ground state without
changing the boundary conditions~\cite{DIRD_Davis_Vacuum,DIRD_Davis_Tomography}.

\textbf{Assessment:} Casimir-force measurements support boundary-dependent quantum
vacuum stress, and the idealized calculation contains negative renormalized energy
density relative to the exterior vacuum. They do not demonstrate a stockpileable
exotic material or a scalable route to an arbitrarily specified negative
stress-energy tensor. Because proposed warp requirements depend strongly on
metric, profile, speed, and quantum-inequality assumptions, a single
Casimir-to-warp ``gap factor'' is not scientifically
defined~\cite{PfenningFord1997,Casimir1948,Lamoreaux1997,DIRD_Davis_Vacuum,DIRD_Davis_Tomography}.

\subsection{Traversable Wormholes}\label{sec:wormholes}

An alternative to the warp drive is the traversable wormhole---a topological
shortcut through spacetime connecting distant regions. The Morris--Thorne
metric~(1988) provided the standard mathematical framework for a traversable
(non-collapsing) wormhole~\cite{MorrisThorne1988}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:morris-thorne}
  ds^{2} = -e^{2\Phi(r)}\,c^{2}\,dt^{2}
  + \frac{dr^{2}}{1 - b(r)/r}
  + r^{2}\bigl(d\theta^{2} + \sin^{2}\!\theta\;d\phi^{2}\bigr)\,,
\end{equation}
where $\Phi(r)$ is the redshift function and $b(r)$ is the shape function. For
the wormhole throat to remain open, Morris and Thorne showed that exotic matter
violating the null energy condition is required at the
throat~\cite{MorrisThorne1988,Visser1995}.

The ER=EPR conjecture (Maldacena and Susskind, 2013) proposes a deep connection
between Einstein--Rosen bridges (wormholes, ``ER'') and quantum entanglement
(Einstein--Podolsky--Rosen pairs, ``EPR'')~\cite{MaldacenaSusskind2013}. If correct, this
conjecture would imply that wormholes are a natural feature of quantum
gravity---but it remains unproven and would not, by itself, provide a pathway to
macroscopic traversable wormholes.

\textbf{Assessment:} Traversable wormholes are \emph{theoretically valid} within
GR but face the same exotic matter barrier as warp drives. The ER=EPR conjecture
is an active area of theoretical research with \emph{no experimental
verification}~\cite{MorrisThorne1988,MaldacenaSusskind2013,Visser1995}.

\subsection{Deuterium--Tritium Fusion}\label{sec:dt-fusion}

Deuterium--tritium~(DT) fusion is the most experimentally accessible fusion
reaction~\cite{DIRD_Winterberg}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dt-fusion}
  {}^{2}_{1}\text{D} + {}^{3}_{1}\text{T}
  \;\longrightarrow\;
  {}^{4}_{2}\text{He}\;(\SI{3.5}{MeV}) + n\;(\SI{14.1}{MeV})\,.
\end{equation}
The reaction releases \SI{17.6}{MeV} per event ($\SI{2.8e-12}{J}$), with 80\% of
the energy carried by the neutron.

\textbf{Lawson criterion.}\ For ignition---alpha-particle heating alone balancing
energy losses in a 50:50 D--T plasma---the plasma must satisfy the Lawson
criterion:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lawson}
  n\,\tau_{E} \ge \frac{12\,k_{B}\,T}{E_{\mathrm{ch}}\,\langle\sigma v\rangle}\,,
\end{equation}
where $n$ is plasma density, $\tau_{E}$ is energy confinement time, $T$ is plasma
temperature, $E_{\mathrm{ch}}\approx\SI{3.5}{MeV}$ is the charged-particle (alpha)
energy retained in the plasma per reaction (the \SI{14.1}{MeV} neutron escapes),
and $\langle\sigma v\rangle$ is the temperature-averaged reactivity. The minimum is
$n\tau_{E}\approx\SI{1.5e20}{s/m^{3}}$ near \SI{25}{keV}; the triple product
$nT\tau_{E}\gtrsim\SI{3e21}{keV.s/m^{3}}$ is minimized near \SI{14}{keV}
(${\approx}\SI{1.6e8}{K}$), where $n\tau_{E}\approx\SI{2e20}{s/m^{3}}$.

\textbf{Current status.}\ The National Ignition Facility~(NIF) at Lawrence
Livermore National Laboratory achieved target gain~$>1$ on December~5, 2022, with
\SI{2.05}{MJ} of laser energy producing \SI{3.15}{MJ} of fusion output. LLNL
reported eleven ignition experiments through June~20, 2026 (post-cutoff update,
checked 2026-08-02); the record listed on its ignition page remains the April~7,
2025 shot at \SI{8.6}{MJ} output from \SI{2.08}{MJ} delivered to the
target~\cite{LLNL_NIF2025}. Representative shots are listed in

\subsection{DD and Aneutronic Fusion}\label{sec:dd-aneutronic}

\textbf{DD fusion} eliminates the tritium supply bottleneck (global tritium
production is ${\sim}\SI{4}{kg/year}$ from CANDU reactors, with a 12.3-year
half-life). The DD reaction proceeds through two channels with approximately equal
probability:
\begin{align}
  {}^{2}_{1}\text{D} + {}^{2}_{1}\text{D}
  &\;\longrightarrow\;
  {}^{3}_{2}\text{He}\;(\SI{0.82}{MeV}) + n\;(\SI{2.45}{MeV})\,,
  \label{eq:dd1}\\
  {}^{2}_{1}\text{D} + {}^{2}_{1}\text{D}
  &\;\longrightarrow\;
  {}^{3}_{1}\text{T}\;(\SI{1.01}{MeV}) + p\;(\SI{3.02}{MeV})\,.
  \label{eq:dd2}
\end{align}
DD fusion requires approximately $10\times$ higher plasma temperatures than DT
(${\sim}10^{9}\;\text{K}$ vs.\ ${\sim}10^{8}\;\text{K}$), making it substantially
more challenging~\cite{DIRD_Winterberg}.

\textbf{Aneutronic fusion}---particularly proton--boron-11
(p--$^{11}$B)---has a primary reaction branch that produces charged alpha
particles rather than neutrons, enabling a possible route to direct energy
conversion~\cite{DIRD_Teofilo}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pb11}
  p + {}^{11}_{5}\text{B}
  \;\longrightarrow\;
  3\;{}^{4}_{2}\text{He} + \SI{8.7}{MeV}\,.
\end{equation}
However, p--$^{11}$B requires temperatures approximately $10\times$ higher than
D--T (${\sim}10^{9}\;\text{K}$), has a much lower effective reactivity under
reactor conditions, and still produces a residual neutron field through secondary
reactions and impurities. It is therefore more accurate to call the primary branch
aneutronic than to claim zero neutrons.

\subsection{Heavy Water as Fuel}\label{sec:heavy-water}

Deuterium is one of the most abundant isotopes in the universe. In Earth's oceans,
approximately 1 in every 6\,420 hydrogen atoms is deuterium
($\text{D}/\text{H}\approx156$~ppm), yielding ${\sim}\SI{34}{g}$ of deuterium per
cubic metre of seawater. The world's oceans contain approximately
$\SI{4.6e13}{tonnes}$ of deuterium---enough to power DD fusion for billions of
years at current global energy consumption rates.

Heavy water~(D$_{2}$O)---water in which both hydrogen atoms are replaced by
deuterium---is the most convenient form for deuterium storage and extraction. It
is produced industrially via the Girdler sulfide process and electrolysis, with
global production capacity of hundreds of tonnes per year.

\textbf{Historical note:} The strategic importance of heavy water was recognized
during the Manhattan Project and the Norwegian heavy water sabotage operations of
1942--1944. Today, heavy water serves as a neutron moderator in CANDU fission
reactors and as a potential fuel feedstock for future fusion systems.

The transition from chemical to nuclear energy in propulsion echoes a long arc of
engineering history. Hero of Alexandria's aeolipile (1st century CE)---a simple
steam-powered rotating sphere---contained the essential principle of reaction
propulsion, yet nearly two millennia elapsed before the steam turbine became a
practical power source. Similarly, the physics of nuclear fusion is well
understood, but the engineering required to harness it for compact propulsion
systems remains formidable.

\subsection{Compact Fusion Concepts}\label{sec:compact-fusion}

Several approaches to compact fusion are under active
development~\cite{DIRD_Miley,DIRD_Teofilo}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Magnetized target fusion~(MTF):} General Fusion---uses pistons to
        compress a magnetized plasma target.
  \item \textbf{Field-reversed configuration~(FRC):} TAE Technologies---confines
        plasma in a self-organizing magnetic structure.
  \item \textbf{Z-pinch:} Zap Energy---uses electric current through plasma to
        create self-confining magnetic fields.
  \item \textbf{Inertial electrostatic confinement~(IEC):} Explored in
        DIRD~\#9 (Miley)~\cite{DIRD_Miley}---uses electrostatic fields to
        accelerate and confine ions.
\end{itemize}
None of these approaches have demonstrated net energy production as of 2026.
The path from laboratory demonstration to a compact, mobile fusion reactor
suitable for aerospace propulsion remains long and
uncertain~\cite{DIRD_Miley,DIRD_Teofilo,LLNL_NIF2025}.

\subsection{Exotic Energy Sources}\label{sec:exotic-energy}

\textbf{Vacuum energy.} Quantum field theory predicts that the quantum vacuum
possesses a non-zero energy density. The naive calculation with a Planck-scale
cutoff yields a spectacularly large value---of order the Planck energy density,
${\sim}\SI{4.6e113}{J/m^{3}}$---roughly $10^{123}$ times the observed dark-energy density
(${\sim}\SI{6e-10}{J/m^{3}}$); the commonly quoted ``${\sim}10^{120}$''
discrepancy depends on the cutoff convention. This discrepancy, known as the
``cosmological constant problem,'' is one of the deepest unsolved problems in
physics.

Even if vacuum energy could be extracted, fundamental thermodynamic arguments
suggest that the vacuum---as the lowest energy state---cannot serve as a
conventional energy source. DIRD~\#24 (Davis) and DIRD~\#36 (Davis, ``Quantum
Tomography of Negative Energy States in the Vacuum'') explored this question
without identifying a practical extraction
mechanism~\cite{DIRD_Davis_Vacuum,DIRD_Davis_Tomography}.

\textbf{Assessment:} Fusion is a well-established nuclear energy process and a
legitimate candidate for future high-power propulsion studies. No compact mobile
reactor has demonstrated net electric power, and fusion falls many orders of
magnitude short of the source requirements in published strongly curved warp
examples. It should not be described as the demonstrated or uniquely most
plausible power source for metric engineering. Vacuum energy extraction faces
fundamental theoretical obstacles and is
\emph{speculative}~\cite{DIRD_Miley,DIRD_Davis_Vacuum,DIRD_Davis_Tomography,DIRD_Teofilo,LLNL_NIF2025}.

\section{Electromagnetic Signatures}\label{sec:em-signatures}

\subsection{Model-Dependent Quantum-Field Signatures}\label{sec:em-prediction}

Quantum-field radiation is not determined by the phrase ``spacetime
manipulation.'' A calculation must specify the time-dependent metric or material
boundary, quantum field, initial state, observer, and detector response. The
laboratory and curved-spacetime effects below are established in their own
regimes, but they do not supply a universal UAP spectrum.

\textbf{The Dynamical Casimir Effect (DCE).}\ Rapidly changing electromagnetic
boundary conditions can mix field modes and produce correlated photons. Wilson et
al.~(2011) demonstrated microwave DCE radiation in a superconducting transmission
line whose electrical boundary condition was deliberately modulated with a
SQUID~\cite{Wilson2011}. Photon rate and spectrum depend on the apparatus, drive
waveform, resonances, losses, and field state; a simplified
effective-boundary-velocity scaling is not a universal emission
law~\cite{Wilson2011,Schutzhold2011}.

An Alcubierre bubble wall is a metric gradient, not automatically a conducting or
reflecting electromagnetic boundary. The cited DCE papers study superconducting
circuits or laser-driven refractive-index perturbations and do not derive
microwave, terahertz, or X-ray emission from a warp
metric~\cite{Alcubierre1994,Wilson2011,Schutzhold2011}.

\textbf{The Unruh Effect.}\ A uniformly accelerating observer has an associated
temperature~\cite{Unruh1976}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unruh}
  T_{\text{Unruh}} = \frac{\hbar\,a}{2\pi\,c\,k_{B}}\,,
\end{equation}
where $a$ is that observer's \emph{proper} acceleration. Substituting the
source-dependent $5{,}370\,g$ coordinate-motion scenario gives
${\sim}\SI{2e-16}{K}$ only if it is also the observer's proper acceleration. A
geodesic observer instead has $a=0$. ``Planck-scale effective acceleration'' is not
a defined input and supplies no detector prediction~\cite{Unruh1976}.

\textbf{Hawking-like radiation.}\ Hawking radiation is associated with a horizon
and quantum state, not curvature in general~\cite{Hawking1974,Hawking1975}. For a
black hole of mass $M$ the Hawking temperature is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hawking}
  T_{\text{Hawking}} = \frac{\hbar\,c^{3}}{8\pi\,G\,M\,k_{B}}\,.
\end{equation}
Specific dynamically formed \emph{superluminal} warp metrics can possess horizons
and have been calculated to generate Hawking-like flux and semiclassical
instability, with the spectrum controlled by surface gravity. A generic subluminal
bubble has no such warp horizon, so black-hole mass--temperature formulas such as
Eq.~\eqref{eq:hawking} cannot be transferred to
it~\cite{Alcubierre1994,Schutzhold2011,Finazzi2009}.

\textbf{Synthesis:} DCE, Unruh, and Hawking radiation involve different physical
setups and observer/state dependence. Analogies among them can guide explicit
calculations but do not establish that a hypothetical engine emits
electromagnetic radiation, much less a unique broadband
fingerprint~\cite{Wilson2011,Hawking1974,Hawking1975,Unruh1976,Schutzhold2011}.

\subsection{Terahertz Radiation Analysis}\label{sec:thz}

The terahertz frequency band (\SIrange{0.1}{10}{THz}, wavelength
\SI{30}{\micro\metre}--\SI{3}{\milli\metre}) occupies the ``THz gap'' between
microwave electronics and infrared photonics. Both generation and detection of THz
radiation remain technically challenging, which means:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a source emitting primarily in the THz band could be missed by sensor
        suites without that coverage;
  \item purpose-built THz detectors may add useful information when integrated
        with calibrated multi-band instruments;
  \item no parameter set in the cited warp literature establishes a preferential
        \SIrange{0.1}{10}{THz} band.
\end{enumerate}

THz instrumentation can close a real sensor-coverage gap, but no cited warp
calculation predicts a THz flux above detector backgrounds. THz observations should
therefore be justified as broad spectral characterization, not as a privileged DCE
or warp-propulsion test~\cite{Wilson2011,Schutzhold2011}.

\subsection{Electronic Interference}\label{sec:electronic-interference}

Reported electromagnetic interference effects near UAP---including vehicle engine
failure, radio disruption, compass deviation, and electronic equipment
malfunction---are anecdotal, and if real they would require different physical
agents: compass deviation requires a quasi-static magnetic field comparable to
Earth's ${\sim}25$--\SI{65}{\micro\tesla} field at the compass, whereas radio
disruption and electronic upset can be produced by sufficiently strong
radiofrequency or microwave fields~\cite{DIRD_Green}.

\textbf{Electromagnetic pulse~(EMP)} effects on electronics are thoroughly studied
in the context of nuclear weapons effects and directed energy weapons. High-power
microwave~(HPM) sources can disable electronic systems at ranges of hundreds of
meters to kilometers. The U.S.\ military has developed and deployed HPM weapons
(e.g., the Counter-electronics High Power Microwave Advanced Missile Project,
CHAMP).

Sufficiently strong conventional electromagnetic fields can disrupt electronics,
but no cited warp calculation predicts such a field or dose. Electronic-interference
reports therefore cannot be used to infer DCE or spacetime
manipulation~\cite{Wilson2011,Schutzhold2011}.

\textbf{Assessment:} Reported electronic interference is compatible with several
conventional causes, including electromagnetic fields, equipment faults,
environmental conditions, and reporting error. Attribution requires synchronized
field measurements and instrument diagnostics; compatibility alone is not evidence
of a warp-related source.

\section{Advanced Materials and Structural Analysis}\label{sec:materials}

\subsection{Metamaterials}\label{sec:metamaterials}

Metamaterials are artificially structured materials whose electromagnetic
properties arise from their geometry rather than their chemical composition. The
field was founded on Veselago's theoretical prediction~(1968) of materials with
simultaneously negative permittivity~($\varepsilon$) and
permeability~($\mu$)~\cite{Veselago1968}, experimentally realized by Smith et
al.~(2000)~\cite{Smith2000}.

Key demonstrated properties include negative refractive index (light bends in the
opposite direction from conventional materials), a reversed Doppler effect
(frequency shifts are inverted for objects moving relative to the medium),
reversed Cherenkov radiation (experimentally confirmed in 2017), and
sub-diffraction-limit imaging: Pendry's ``perfect lens''~(2000) showed that a
negative-index slab amplifies evanescent waves, enabling imaging below the
diffraction limit~\cite{Pendry2000}.

\textbf{Transformation optics} (Pendry, Schurig \& Smith, 2006) provides the
mathematical framework for electromagnetic cloaking: by prescribing a coordinate
transformation, one can determine the metamaterial parameters needed to guide EM
waves around an object~\cite{PendrySchurig2006}. The required material parameters
are derived from the Jacobian of the transformation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transformation-optics}
\begin{aligned}
  \varepsilon'^{ij} &= [\det(\Lambda)]^{-1}\,
    \Lambda^{i}{}_{k}\,\Lambda^{j}{}_{l}\,\varepsilon^{kl}\,,\\
  \mu'^{ij} &= [\det(\Lambda)]^{-1}\,
    \Lambda^{i}{}_{k}\,\Lambda^{j}{}_{l}\,\mu^{kl}\,,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\Lambda^{i}{}_{k} = \partial x'^{i}/\partial x^{k}$ is the coordinate
transformation Jacobian; for a cloak designed from free space
($\varepsilon=\mu=1$ in relative units), $\varepsilon'=\mu'$.

\textbf{Current limitations:} Transformation-optics cloaking was first
demonstrated at microwave frequencies for a two-dimensional object (Schurig et
al.\ 2006), and reduced-parameter ``carpet'' cloaks have since been shown at
near-infrared and optical wavelengths for small objects on a reflecting surface.
Full-parameter optical cloaks require nanometre-scale features and suffer severe
absorption near resonance. More fundamentally, passivity and causality bound the
bandwidth and scattering reduction achievable by any passive, linear cloak, and
the bound tightens as the object grows relative to the wavelength (Monticone \&
Al\`u
2016)~\cite{Shelby2001,PendrySchurig2006,DIRD_Shvets,Schurig2006,MonticoneAlu2016}.

\textbf{Relevance to UAP:} Metamaterials are a credible route to reduced
signatures in selected bands, but broadband visible-light invisibility of a
vehicle-sized object with passive materials is limited by these physical bounds,
not merely by current capabilities.

\subsection{Extreme-Strength Materials}\label{sec:extreme-materials}

Whatever the propulsion hypothesis, a vehicle's structure must carry the loads it
actually experiences: proper acceleration during any non-geodesic phase, tidal
(geodesic-deviation) stresses across its finite size even on a geodesic worldline
(Key Judgment~\ref{kj:tidal}), and pressure and thermal loads wherever it
interacts with air or water. Because none of these load cases is measured for UAP
(Sec.~\ref{sec:materials-gap}), the figures below describe present materials
capability, not a demonstrated requirement.

\textbf{Current state of the art:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Carbon nanotubes~(CNTs):} theoretical tensile strength
        ${\sim}100$--\SI{200}{GPa}; centimetre-long bundles of ultralong CNTs
        reached ${\sim}\SI{80}{GPa}$ on the nanotube-wall cross-section
        (${\approx}\SI{43}{GPa}$ engineering strength) (Bai et al.,
        2018)~\cite{Bai2018}. Young's modulus ${\sim}\SI{1}{TPa}$. Specific
        strength is of order $10^{4}$~kN\,m/kg---e.g., ${\sim}36{,}000$~kN\,m/kg
        for \SI{80}{GPa} on a wall cross-section of graphitic density
        (${\sim}\SI{2200}{kg/m^{3}}$), or the often-quoted
        ${\sim}48{,}000$~kN\,m/kg for \SI{63}{GPa} at a bulk density of
        ${\sim}\SI{1300}{kg/m^{3}}$---roughly 200--$300\times$ high-carbon steel
        (${\sim}154$~kN\,m/kg).
  \item \textbf{Graphene:} monolayer tensile strength ${\sim}\SI{130}{GPa}$;
        Young's modulus ${\sim}\SI{1}{TPa}$~\cite{Lee2008}.
  \item \textbf{Ideal-strength estimate:} the ideal (theoretical) strength of a
        defect-free crystal is of order $E/10$, where $E$ is the Young's
        modulus---an order-of-magnitude estimate set by interatomic bonding, not
        a strict upper bound. For sp$^{2}$ carbon ($E\sim\SI{1}{TPa}$) it gives
        ${\sim}\SI{100}{GPa}$, comparable to the measured intrinsic strength of
        defect-free graphene (${\sim}\SI{130}{GPa}\approx E/8$).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The practical gap:} Macroscale carbon nanotube fibers achieve only a few
GPa due to defects, weak inter-tube shear interactions, and processing
limitations. Even individual, structure-defined single-walled CNTs measured only
${\sim}25$--\SI{66}{GPa}, well below the 100--\SI{200}{GPa} ideal predictions,
with strength depending on chirality and on stress concentration at unavoidable
structural defects. Bridging the gap between single-tube and bulk performance
remains a central challenge in materials science.

\subsection{Bismuth--Magnesium Analysis}\label{sec:bismuth-mg}

The so-called ``Art's Parts''---bismuth--magnesium layered specimens claimed to
have been recovered from a 1947 UAP event---have been the subject of considerable
public attention.

\textbf{AARO/ORNL analysis (contracted 2022; ORNL synopsis April~2024; AARO
supplement July~2024).} The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office contracted Oak
Ridge National Laboratory~(ORNL) to analyze the specimen---a single parent sample
and three previously derived subsamples of the same material, made available
through DEVCOM from February~2023. Key findings~\cite{AARO_ORNL2024}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the material is a magnesium alloy containing zinc, bismuth, lead, and
        trace elements;
  \item the bismuth-rich layers are nanocrystalline/amorphous and co-located with
        lead in a ${\sim}1{:}1$ ratio, not the pure single-crystalline bismuth
        layer postulated for a terahertz waveguide;
  \item multiple repeating Bi--Pb layers, rather than a single layer, would likely
        interfere with rather than enable waveguide function; magnesium and lead
        isotope ratios fall within terrestrial ranges;
  \item ``all data strongly support that the material is terrestrial in
        origin.''
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Proposed industrial analogy:} The Betterton--Kroll process removes bismuth
from molten lead by adding calcium and magnesium, which form a
calcium--magnesium bismuthide dross; it has been informally suggested as a
possible context. Neither the ORNL synopsis nor AARO's July~2024 supplement
mentions it, and the specimen's composition (${\sim}97.5\%$ Mg, ${\sim}2\%$ Zn,
with Pb and Bi each only ${\sim}0.3$--$0.4\%$ in co-located bands) has not been
compared with such dross. AARO instead found the specimen consistent with
mid-20th-century Mg--Zn alloy research (including studies of Pb and Bi additives)
and assessed it as likely a test object, a manufacturing product or byproduct, or
a component of aerospace materials studies~\cite{AARO_ORNL2024}.

\textbf{Assessment:} ORNL assessed with high confidence that the tested specimen
was manufactured terrestrially and found a bismuth THz-waveguide function highly
unlikely. Claims of a non-terrestrial or advanced metamaterial function are not
supported by the tested sample, while chain of custody and original purpose remain
unresolved.

\subsection{Subatomic Bonding Concepts}\label{sec:subatomic-bonding}

The book that inspired this report discusses the concept of ``subatomic
bonds''---bonding mechanisms at scales below the atomic level that could yield
materials with extraordinary properties.

\textbf{Strong nuclear force basics.} The strong interaction, described by quantum
chromodynamics~(QCD), operates between quarks via gluon exchange. Its key feature
is confinement: quarks cannot exist in isolation and are permanently bound into
hadrons (protons, neutrons, and other composite particles). The QCD coupling runs
with energy scale and becomes non-perturbative near confinement. Comparing a rough
order-unity QCD coupling with the electromagnetic fine-structure constant does not
yield a ratio of bond or material strengths.

\textbf{Quark matter.} At temperatures of roughly $1.8\times10^{12}$~K
($T\approx\SI{155}{MeV}$) and low net-baryon density---and possibly at several
(${\sim}5$--10) times nuclear density in cold matter---hadronic matter converts to
a quark-gluon plasma~(QGP) in which quarks and gluons are deconfined; lattice QCD
shows that at near-zero baryon density this is a smooth crossover rather than a
sharp phase transition. QGP has been produced at RHIC (Brookhaven) and the LHC
(CERN), but only for ${\sim}10^{-23}\;\text{s}$ in heavy-ion collisions.

\textbf{Strange matter hypothesis.} Witten~(1984) and Bodmer~(1971) proposed that
``strange matter''---matter containing roughly equal numbers of up, down, and
strange quarks---might be the absolute ground state of strongly interacting
matter~\cite{Witten1984,Bodmer1971}. If true, strange matter would be more stable
than ordinary nuclear matter and could exist in macroscopic quantities (strange
stars). The hypothesis remains unverified.

\textbf{Relevance to materials:} Nuclear binding energies are typically MeV per
nucleon whereas chemical bonds are eV-scale, but nuclear interactions act over
femtometre distances and stable ordinary solids are organized by electromagnetic
bonding. Neither the energy-scale comparison nor a coupling-constant ratio predicts
macroscopic tensile strength. No cited source supports a material that is
``$100\times$ stronger'' because it uses subatomic
bonding~\cite{Witten1984,Bodmer1971}.

\textbf{Assessment:} While the strong force is \emph{verified} physics, the
concept of macroscopic materials bonded by subatomic forces is \emph{speculative}
with no known physical mechanism for realization.

\section{Integrated Propulsion Model --- Synthesis}\label{sec:synthesis}

\subsection{How the Pieces Fit}\label{sec:pieces}

The preceding sections do not establish a unified propulsion model. They identify
the minimum calculations that any metric-engineering hypothesis would have to
supply, in sequence:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item calibrated observations with uncertainties;
  \item an explicit target metric and formation history;
  \item a conserved supporting stress-energy $T_{\mu\nu}$, from which each of the
        following must then be calculated:
        \begin{itemize}
          \item the reference worldline and finite-body tidal loads;
          \item matter coupling, shocks, heating, drag, and wake;
          \item null-geodesic images for defined observers;
          \item the quantum-field state and model-specific radiation;
          \item stability, control, shutdown, and environmental effects.
        \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

General relativity already couples energy and momentum to curvature. The missing
step is not a separate ``energy conversion'' interaction but a physically
realizable, conserved, stable, and controllable source with the required sign,
anisotropy, gradients, and dynamics. Fusion power, a Casimir cavity, and a
mathematical metric cannot simply be concatenated into an engine; each step above
requires its own quantitative model.

\subsection{Energy Budget Analysis}\label{sec:energy-budget}

There is no geometry-independent energy budget for a ``10-meter warp bubble.'' A
budget must state the metric, speed profile, wall function and thickness,
stress-energy definition, formation history, and desired observable effect.

Bobrick and Martire construct a family continuous from near-Minkowski material
shells to more strongly curved configurations. Their Earth-mass/\SI{10}{m} example
produces only a small clock-rate change and is not a universal planetary-mass
lower bound~\cite{Alcubierre1994,BobrickMartire2021}. By contrast, Pfenning and
Ford's quantum-inequality analysis of a specific Alcubierre profile gives a wall of
roughly a few hundred Planck lengths and, for a \SI{100}{m}-radius example,
$E/c^{2}\sim-6.2\times10^{62}\,v_{b}$~kg
($|E|\sim5.6\times10^{79}\,v_{b}$~J), where $v_{b}$ is the bubble speed in units
of $c$. These numbers describe different constructions and must not be combined
into a universal target density~\cite{PfenningFord1997}.

\textbf{Assessment:} Strongly curved published examples can have fantastically
large and/or negative source requirements, but no single number applies to all
subluminal metrics. Energy claims must remain model-dependent and should not be
presented as a fusion-reactor sizing exercise.

\section{Intelligence Gaps and Research Directions}\label{sec:research}

\subsection{Key Unknowns}\label{sec:unknowns}

The following represent the most significant gaps in our understanding:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Can a physically admissible target stress-energy be realized and
        controlled?} Casimir experiments validate boundary-dependent vacuum
        stress in specific geometries but do not supply a configurable
        macroscopic source.
  \item \textbf{Can warp-type transport be sourced by positive energy?}
        Lentz~(2021) and Fell and Heisenberg~(2021) claimed superluminal
        positive-energy solitons. Santiago, Schuster, and Visser~(2022) showed
        that such Nat\'ario-class constructions still violate the weak energy
        condition and, under mild assumptions, the null energy condition. Fuchs
        et al.~(2024) constructed a constant-velocity subluminal shell that
        satisfies all point-wise energy conditions, but no positive-energy
        accelerating or superluminal solution is known. Whether modified gravity
        relaxes these constraints remains open.
  \item \textbf{What is the actual electromagnetic spectrum of UAP?}
        High-quality, calibrated, multi-spectral sensor data from UAP encounters
        is almost nonexistent. Without this data, all propulsion hypotheses remain
        underdetermined.
  \item \textbf{What metric and source are actually being proposed?} Without a
        target metric, conserved $T_{\mu\nu}$, formation history, and desired
        observable effect, there is no geometry-independent energy requirement to
        compare with fusion or any other source.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Recommended Observation Priorities}\label{sec:observation}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Deploy calibrated, synchronized multi-sensor systems} with
        complete timing, geometry, environmental data, raw measurements,
        uncertainty budgets, and chain of custody. Add THz coverage where
        practical as one band among many; no privileged warp frequency is
        predicted.
  \item \textbf{Calibrate multi-spectral imaging} across the full EM spectrum
        (radio through gamma) for high-temporal-resolution observation.
  \item \textbf{Define detector sensitivity before deployment.} Gravimeters or
        strain sensors are useful only when a candidate model predicts an
        amplitude, waveform, bandwidth, range, and background-discrimination
        criterion.
  \item \textbf{Collect environmental samples under a preregistered protocol}
        with blanks, controls, custody records, and baseline distributions.
        Isotope anomalies alone would not identify fusion or a propulsion
        mechanism.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Promising Research Avenues}\label{sec:avenues}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Explicit quantum-field calculations:} For any proposed
        time-dependent metric, specify the field, state, boundary conditions,
        observer, spectrum, and flux before selecting a detector band. Laboratory
        DCE remains an independent quantum-electronics topic, not a validated
        proxy for warp propulsion.
  \item \textbf{Modified gravity theories:} Frameworks beyond standard GR (e.g.,
        $f(R)$ gravity, scalar-tensor theories) may relax energy conditions for
        warp geometries. This is an active area of theoretical research.
  \item \textbf{Compact fusion:} While the path to commercial fusion is long, even
        partial success in compact reactor design (e.g., HTS tokamaks) would
        dramatically advance the energy-density frontier.
  \item \textbf{Analog-gravity systems:} Metamaterials and fluids can test the
        dynamics of their stated analog model. They do not reproduce
        Einstein-equation sourcing and cannot by themselves validate a propulsion
        interpretation~\cite{Wilson2011,PendrySchurig2006,Lahteenmaki2013,Steinhauer2016}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What Would Constitute Definitive Evidence}\label{sec:evidence}

For a spacetime manipulation propulsion system:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A preregistered, metric-specific prediction of kinematics, curvature,
        matter coupling, and any quantum-field spectrum before an event.
  \item Repeatable measurements matching those quantitative predictions across
        independent, calibrated instruments with adequate sensitivity.
  \item A physically characterized source whose measured stress-energy and
        dynamics generate the predicted geometry while satisfying conservation and
        stated stability conditions.
  \item Independent replication with raw data, metadata, uncertainty propagation,
        and conventional alternatives tested.
\end{enumerate}

For establishing UAP as beyond-current-technology:
\begin{enumerate}
  \setcounter{enumi}{4}
  \item Confirmed acceleration exceeding $1{,}000\,g$ by multiple independent,
        calibrated sensor systems.
  \item Confirmed transmedium transition observed simultaneously by above-surface
        and below-surface sensors.
  \item Material recovery with secure chain of custody and reproducible
        properties inconsistent with terrestrial processes (ORNL assessed the
        tested ``Art's Parts'' specimen as terrestrially manufactured with high
        confidence, while its original purpose remains unresolved).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical and Relativistic Foundations for Anomalous Flight}\label{sec:mathfoundations}

\subsection{Invariant Kinematics}\label{sec:invariant-kinematics}

Any physically serious theory of anomalous flight should start from invariant
quantities rather than raw image-plane or lab-frame acceleration. The relevant
object is a timelike worldline $x^\mu(\tau)$ with
\begin{equation}
u^\mu = \frac{dx^\mu}{d\tau},\qquad
u_\mu u^\mu = -c^2,\qquad
a^\mu = u^\nu \nabla_\nu u^\mu,
\label{eq:s09-kinematics}
\end{equation}
and proper acceleration
\begin{equation}
\alpha = \sqrt{a_\mu a^\mu}.
\label{eq:s09-proper-acceleration}
\end{equation}
The onboard inertial load is set by $\alpha$, not by the coordinate acceleration
inferred by a remote observer. This distinction is central: a vehicle following
a geodesic can display extreme coordinate motion while its occupants experience
zero proper acceleration~\cite{MTW1973,Wald1984,Touboul2022}.

\subsection{A Minimal Metric Ansatz}\label{sec:minimal-metric}

The natural mathematical language is the $3+1$ decomposition of spacetime,
\begin{equation}
ds^2 = -(N^2-\gamma_{ij}\beta^i\beta^j)c^2dt^2
+ 2\gamma_{ij}\beta^j c\,dt\,dx^i
+ \gamma_{ij}dx^i dx^j,
\label{eq:s09-adm}
\end{equation}
where $N$ is the lapse, $\beta^i$ the shift vector, and $\gamma_{ij}$ the
spatial metric. A compact toy model remains the Alcubierre family,
\begin{equation}
ds^2 = -c^2 dt^2 + \left(dx - v_s(t) f(r_s)\,dt\right)^2 + dy^2 + dz^2,
\label{eq:s09-alcubierre}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
r_s &= \sqrt{(x-x_s(t))^2+y^2+z^2},\\
f(r_s) &= \frac{\tanh[\sigma(r_s+R)]-\tanh[\sigma(r_s-R)]}
{2\tanh(\sigma R)}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:s09-shape}
\end{equation}
This isolates a small set of tunable geometric parameters: bubble radius $R$,
wall sharpness $\Sigma = \sigma R \sim R/\Delta$, and speed profile
$v_s(t)$~\cite{Alcubierre1994,MTW1973,Wald1984}.

\subsection{Stress-Energy as the Core Constraint}\label{sec:stress-energy-core}

A propulsion hypothesis only becomes physics once its supporting stress-energy
is specified through the Einstein field equations,
\begin{equation}
G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} T_{\mu\nu}.
\label{eq:s09-einstein}
\end{equation}
For Eulerian observers, the local energy density is
$\rho_E = T_{\mu\nu}n^\mu n^\nu$. For the Alcubierre metric, Alcubierre showed
that
\begin{equation}
\rho_E = -\frac{c^{2}v_s^{2}}{32\pi G}\,\frac{y^{2}+z^{2}}{r_s^{2}}
\left(\frac{df}{dr_s}\right)^{2} < 0
\label{eq:s09-rhoE}
\end{equation}
wherever the wall gradient is nonzero. Pfenning and Ford then showed that quantum
inequalities confine this negative energy to an extremely thin wall with an
enormous integrated magnitude, and Santiago, Schuster, and Visser showed that
generic warp drives violate the null energy condition. In other words, a
mathematically explicit UAP transport model must declare up front whether it lives
in an exotic-matter regime or in a conservative positive-energy
regime~\cite{Alcubierre1994,PfenningFord1997,SantiagoVisser2022}.

\subsection{Conservative and Exotic Branches}\label{sec:branches}

The literature now supports a useful bifurcation. In the conservative branch,
the goal is positive-energy spacetime control and one accepts subluminal
transport as in Bobrick and Martire's ``physical warp drives'' and the explicit
constant-velocity shell of Fuchs et al.~(2024). In the exotic branch, one allows
energy-condition violation to preserve the superluminal idealization, but then
inherits the negative-energy and controllability burdens already identified in the
classical warp-drive literature. Positive-energy escape routes proposed in more
speculative geometries remain mathematically interesting, but not yet
consensus~\cite{BobrickMartire2021,FellHeisenberg2021,Fuchs2024}.

\subsection{Dimensionless Control Parameters}\label{sec:dimensionless-parameters}

For future model-building, the most useful ``constants'' are dimensionless
ratios rather than a single fitted number:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\beta_s &= \frac{v_s}{c},\qquad
\Sigma = \sigma R \sim \frac{R}{\Delta},\\
\mathcal{A} &= \frac{\alpha R}{c^2},\qquad
\mathcal{K} = K_{\max}R^4,\\
\Xi &= \frac{G|E|}{R c^4}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:s09-dimensionless}
\end{equation}
These respectively capture apparent transport speed, wall sharpness, onboard
inertial load, curvature strength through the Kretschmann scalar
$K = R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$, and energy compactness. As a
report-specific diagnostic we define a geometric relief factor
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{\mathrm{rel}} = \frac{a_{\mathrm{app}}}{\alpha},
\label{eq:s09-relief}
\end{equation}
where $a_{\mathrm{app}}$ is the coordinate acceleration of the reference worldline
in the frame of a specified external observer. $\Gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}$ measures how
much apparent maneuvering is offloaded from proper acceleration into geometry. It
is observer-dependent and diverges for exactly geodesic transport ($\alpha=0$); in
that limit one should quote the reciprocal $\alpha/a_{\mathrm{app}}$, which
vanishes. A successful anomalous-flight theory would aim for
$\Gamma_{\mathrm{rel}} \gg 1$ (equivalently $\alpha/a_{\mathrm{app}}\ll1$)%
~\cite{Alcubierre1994,PfenningFord1997,SantiagoVisser2022,BobrickMartire2021,MTW1973,Wald1984}.

\subsection{Implication for the UAP Question}\label{sec:uap-question}

This framework does not prove that any UAP employs metric engineering. What it
does provide is a disciplined mathematical filter. Any future theory intended to
explain reported UFO behavior should: (1) keep the craft on a timelike
worldline, (2) bound proper acceleration, (3) specify the required
stress-energy, (4) state explicitly which energy conditions are preserved or
violated, and (5) expose a small set of dimensionless parameters that can later
be compared against observations. That turns the UFO question from a narrative
claim into a falsifiable relativistic program.

\subsection{Layered Field-Shell Transport as a Conservative Particular Case}\label{sec:lfsht}

One cautious way to extend the framework is to treat recurrent close-encounter
motifs such as localized silence or pressure, small luminous spheres,
suspension inside a bounded volume, temporary respiratory support, and larger
carrier-like craft not as measurements, but as phenomenological boundary
conditions for model construction. NASA's official 2023 UAP study emphasizes
that current public cases remain too weakly calibrated and too poorly
instrumented for definitive physical inference, so narrative reports can at
most motivate candidate structures rather than establish them. The motif list is
drawn from narrative literature (principally Fowler, \textit{The Watchers~II},
1995) and an unpublished, author-compiled summary of abduction and craft
descriptors; neither is a calibrated, peer-reviewed, or publicly reproducible
dataset~\cite{Fowler1995,NASA_UAP2023}.

As a conservative particular case, consider a habitable core enclosed by a thin
transport shell and optionally nested within a larger carrier platform. In ADM
variables one imposes an approximately uniform interior lapse and nearly
Euclidean spatial metric,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
N(\mathbf{x},t) &\approx N_0,\\
\gamma_{ij}(\mathbf{x},t) &\approx \delta_{ij},\\
\beta^i(\mathbf{x},t) &\approx \beta^i_0(t)\qquad (r<R_c),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:s09-core}
\end{equation}
while confining strong gradients in $N$, $\beta^i$, and $\gamma_{ij}$ to a
shell of thickness $\Delta$. For the Eulerian (normal) observers of the
foliation, whatever the shift, the proper acceleration is
\begin{equation}
a_i = c^{2} D_i \ln N,
\label{eq:s09-lapse}
\end{equation}
so occupants co-moving with the interior Eulerian flow feel no acceleration where
$N$ is spatially uniform, even when the external shell motion is large. This does
not remove the stress-energy burden; it relocates it into the shell geometry and
its source fields~\cite{BobrickMartire2021,MTW1973,Wald1984,Fuchs2024}.

The corresponding effective source can be written schematically as
\begin{equation}
T^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\mu\nu} =
T^{\mathrm{EM}}_{\mu\nu} +
T^{\mathrm{plasma}}_{\mu\nu} +
T^{\mathrm{shell}}_{\mu\nu} +
T^{\mathrm{env}}_{\mu\nu} +
T^{\mathrm{vac}}_{\mu\nu}.
\label{eq:s09-effective-source}
\end{equation}
Here $T^{\mathrm{EM}}_{\mu\nu}$ and $T^{\mathrm{plasma}}_{\mu\nu}$ describe
confining fields and boundary-layer plasma, $T^{\mathrm{shell}}_{\mu\nu}$ the
structural shell, $T^{\mathrm{env}}_{\mu\nu}$ any occupant-support medium, and
$T^{\mathrm{vac}}_{\mu\nu}$ a possible bounded semiclassical correction. The
conservative reading prioritizes positive-energy terms and accepts a
subluminal transport shell in the Bobrick--Martire sense: a material shell
moving inertially and still requiring
propulsion~\cite{BobrickMartire2021,Fuchs2024}.

Under this interpretation, reports of stillness or pressure map to
boundary-layer shielding and altered acoustic propagation; small luminous orbs
map to auxiliary probes or localized field bubbles; suspension with no clear
floor maps to a comoving geodesic interior; and breathable hoods, viscous
``jelly,'' or head heaviness map to an environmental stabilization interface
rather than proof of antigravity. A large cylindrical craft can then be treated
as a carrier-class architecture for power, control, and pod deployment. These
correspondences are deliberately heuristic. They do not validate the narratives,
but they define a non-law-breaking target for future theory: maximize
$\Gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}$ while staying timelike, approximately subluminal, and
as close as possible to positive-energy
sourcing~\cite{DIRD_Green,NASA_UAP2023,Fowler1995}.

\subsection{Phenomenology-Constrained Oblate Shell Geometry}\label{sec:oblate-shell}

If recurrent craft reports are treated as phenomenological boundary conditions
rather than direct measurements, then repeated disc/oval geometry,
seam-minimized metallic surfaces, curved interiors, and luminous bands suggest
that an oblate shell is a more natural ansatz than a purely spherical bubble.
Because apparent shape is an observer-dependent ray-tracing output
(Sec.~\ref{sec:morphology}), reported outlines cannot be read directly as the
shell's aspect ratio; the oblate form is a modeling choice motivated by
narratives, not an inference from images. Let
\begin{equation}
\chi^2 = \frac{x^2+y^2}{R_\perp^2} + \frac{z^2}{R_z^2},\qquad
\varepsilon = \frac{R_z}{R_\perp} < 1,
\label{eq:s09-oblate}
\end{equation}
where $R_\perp$ is the lateral radius, $R_z$ the vertical semi-axis, and
$\varepsilon$ the aspect ratio. A smooth shell profile centered on $\chi=1$ can
be written as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
f(\chi) &= \frac{1}{2}\left[1-\tanh\!\big(\Sigma(\chi-1)\big)\right],\\
\Sigma &\sim \frac{R_\perp}{\Delta},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:s09-oblate-profile}
\end{equation}
with $\Delta$ the equatorial shell thickness (the polar thickness is
$\varepsilon\Delta$, because $\chi$ rescales $z$ by $R_z$). A core-shell metric can
then be parameterized by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
N &= 1 + (N_0-1)f(\chi),\\
\beta^i &= \beta_0^i(t)\,f(\chi),\\
\gamma_{ij} &= \delta_{ij} + 4\,h_{ij}\,f(\chi)\,[1-f(\chi)].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:s09-oblate-ansatz}
\end{equation}
Inside the core $f\approx 1$ (so $N\approx N_0$, $\beta^i\approx\beta_0^i$, and
$\gamma_{ij}\approx\delta_{ij}$, as required in Sec.~\ref{sec:lfsht}), outside
$f\approx 0$, and the strong gradients are localized to the shell. This turns the
assumed shell morphology into a small set of geometric control parameters: aspect
ratio $\varepsilon$, sharpness $\Sigma$, core scale $R_\perp$, and transport shift
$\beta_0^i(t)$~\cite{NASA_UAP2023,BobrickMartire2021,MTW1973,Wald1984}. With
$N_0=1$ and $h_{ij}=0$ the ansatz reduces to the Nat\'ario class (unit lapse, flat
slices), which Santiago, Schuster, and Visser prove violates the weak energy
condition. A positive-energy realization therefore requires a positive-mass shell
with non-unit lapse and/or non-flat spatial metric, as in the constant-velocity
solution of Fuchs et al.~(2024). No energy-condition check of this ansatz has been
performed here, so ``conservative'' denotes a target, not a demonstrated
property~\cite{SantiagoVisser2022,Fuchs2024}.

\subsection{Tidal and Electromagnetic Constraints}\label{sec:tidal-em}

Low proper acceleration is necessary but not sufficient for a habitable
transport core. The Eulerian proper acceleration is $a_i = c^{2} D_i \ln N$, but
survivability also requires bounded tidal forces. In a local orthonormal comoving
frame, geodesic deviation gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{D^2 \xi^{\hat{i}}}{d\tau^2} =
- c^{2} R^{\hat{i}}{}_{\hat{0}\hat{j}\hat{0}}\,\xi^{\hat{j}},
\label{eq:s09-geodesic-deviation}
\end{equation}
so for a characteristic occupant size $L_c$,
\begin{equation}
\Delta a_{\mathrm{tidal}} \lesssim c^2 L_c
\sup \left|R_{\hat{0}\hat{i}\hat{0}\hat{j}}\right|.
\label{eq:s09-tidal}
\end{equation}
A habitable core therefore requires not only $\alpha \lesssim g_\oplus$ (more
generally, $\alpha$ within sustained human tolerance) but also
\begin{equation}
\Theta = \frac{c^2 L_c}{g_\oplus}
\sup \left|R_{\hat{0}\hat{i}\hat{0}\hat{j}}\right| \ll 1,
\label{eq:s09-theta}
\end{equation}
where $\Theta$ is a dimensionless tidal-habitability
parameter~\cite{MTW1973,Wald1984}.

If one also wishes to model buzzing, diffuse glow, or engine disruption without
invoking exotic new forces, the cleanest route is ordinary induction in a
strong EM/plasma boundary layer. Faraday's law implies
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{ind}} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt},\qquad
\Phi_B = \int \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{A},
\label{eq:s09-faraday}
\end{equation}
and a simple disturbance diagnostic is
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{\mathrm{EMI}} = \frac{|\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{ind}}|}{\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{crit}}},
\label{eq:s09-emi}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{crit}}$ is the characteristic upset threshold of
nearby electronics. In this interpretation, localized interference, buzzing,
and luminous boundary effects are candidate byproducts of an intense shell
environment; they do not by themselves establish superluminal
propulsion~\cite{DIRD_Green,NASA_UAP2023}.

\subsection{Missing-Time Decomposition and Non-Claims}\label{sec:missing-time}

Recurring ``missing time'' reports should not be modeled as pure relativistic
time dilation. A more disciplined decomposition is
\begin{equation}
\Delta t_{\mathrm{gap}} =
\Delta t_{\mathrm{geom}} +
\Delta t_{\mathrm{neuro}} +
\Delta t_{\mathrm{recon}},
\label{eq:s09-gap}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\Delta t_{\mathrm{geom}} = \int (1-\Lambda_t)\,dt_\infty,\qquad
\Lambda_t = \frac{d\tau_{\mathrm{in}}}{dt_\infty}.
\label{eq:s09-lambda}
\end{equation}
Here $\Delta t_{\mathrm{geom}}$ is any true clock-rate offset between interior
and exterior time, $\Delta t_{\mathrm{neuro}}$ covers impaired encoding or
retrieval, and $\Delta t_{\mathrm{recon}}$ later retrospective reconstruction.
In the shell ansatz of Secs.~\ref{sec:lfsht}--\ref{sec:oblate-shell}, occupants
co-moving with the interior Eulerian flow have $\Lambda_t = N_0$. A positive-mass
shell gives $N_0<1$, with $1-N_0$ of order the compactness $\Xi$. The
constant-velocity positive-energy shell of Fuchs et al.~(2024) has
$GM/(Rc^{2})\approx0.17$ at its outer radius, so $N_0\lesssim0.82$. The geometric
term is therefore at most a fractional clock-rate offset of order $\Xi$ during the
encounter, and $\Lambda_t\approx1$ holds only for $\Xi\ll1$. Apparent gaps much
longer than about $\Xi$ times the encounter duration are expected to be dominated
by non-geometric terms. This is consistent both with the transport model above and
with peer-reviewed findings linking some abduction-type reports to sleep paralysis
/ hypnopompic hallucination and to elevated memory-distortion vulnerability in
some abductee-reporting groups. The resulting non-claim is important:
phenomenology alone does not determine
ontology~\cite{NASA_UAP2023,Clancy2002,Cheyne1999,Fuchs2024}.

\section{Glossary}\label{app:glossary}

\begin{description}
  \item[Alcubierre metric] A spacetime metric permitting apparent FTL travel by
    contracting space ahead and expanding it behind a bubble.
  \item[Aneutronic fusion] Fusion reactions whose primary branch releases
    charged particles rather than neutrons (secondary reactions still yield a
    small neutron flux), enabling direct energy conversion.
  \item[Casimir effect] Measurable force between closely spaced conductors due
    to quantum vacuum fluctuations.
  \item[Christoffel symbols] Mathematical objects encoding how coordinate bases
    change across curved spacetime.
  \item[Cosmological constant] The constant $\Lambda$ in Einstein's field
    equations; equivalent to a uniform vacuum energy density
    $\rho_{\Lambda}c^{2}=\Lambda c^{4}/(8\pi G)$.
  \item[Deuterium] Hydrogen isotope with one proton and one neutron.
  \item[Dynamical Casimir effect] Photon pair production from rapidly changing
    vacuum boundary conditions.
  \item[Einstein field equations] The fundamental equations of general relativity
    relating spacetime curvature to matter-energy.
  \item[Equivalence principle] The principle that gravitational and inertial
    acceleration are locally indistinguishable.
  \item[Exotic matter] Matter or fields violating classical energy conditions
    (e.g., negative energy density or null-energy-condition violation); required
    by known superluminal warp-drive solutions.
  \item[Geodesic] The straightest possible path through curved spacetime; objects
    in free fall follow geodesics.
  \item[Gravitational lensing] The bending of light by spacetime curvature.
  \item[Hawking radiation] Thermal radiation emitted by black holes due to
    quantum effects near the event horizon.
  \item[Heavy water (D$_{2}$O)] Water in which hydrogen is replaced by deuterium.
  \item[Hypersonic] Speeds exceeding Mach~5.
  \item[Lawson criterion] The condition for self-sustaining fusion: minimum
    product of density and confinement time.
  \item[Metamaterial] Artificial material with electromagnetic properties
    determined by structure rather than composition.
  \item[Metric tensor] Mathematical object defining distances and angles in
    spacetime.
  \item[Morris--Thorne metric] Mathematical framework for traversable wormholes.
  \item[Null energy condition] The requirement
    $T_{\mu\nu}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}\ge0$ for every null (lightlike) vector $k^{\mu}$;
    for a perfect fluid, $\rho c^{2}+p\ge0$. It is weaker than the weak energy
    condition.
  \item[Proper acceleration] Acceleration measured by an onboard accelerometer.
  \item[QCD] Quantum chromodynamics---the theory of the strong nuclear force
    between quarks.
  \item[Quark-gluon plasma] Deconfined state of matter at extreme
    temperature/density.
  \item[Strange matter] Hypothetical matter containing up, down, and strange
    quarks in equilibrium.
  \item[Stress-energy tensor] Mathematical object encoding the density and flux
    of energy and momentum in spacetime.
  \item[Supercavitation] Creating a gas cavity around an underwater vehicle to
    reduce drag.
  \item[Terahertz (THz)] Electromagnetic frequency band between microwave and
    infrared (\SIrange{0.1}{10}{THz}).
  \item[Transmedium] Capability to operate across multiple physical media (air,
    water, space).
  \item[Traversable wormhole] A topological shortcut through spacetime that
    permits passage.
  \item[Tritium] Hydrogen isotope with one proton and two neutrons.
  \item[UAP] Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (formerly Unidentified Aerial
    Phenomena)---the broad category encompassing unidentified airborne,
    submerged, or transmedium anomalies (objects, sensor returns, optical
    events). The ``aerial'' form was in DoD use by 2020; renamed ``anomalous'' by
    the FY2023 NDAA. See Sec.~\ref{sec:terminology}.
  \item[UFO] Unidentified Flying Object---a subset of UAP referring specifically
    to a discrete physical airborne object of unknown identity
    ($\text{UFO}\subset\text{UAP}$). See Sec.~\ref{sec:terminology}.
  \item[Unruh effect] Thermal radiation perceived by an accelerating observer in
    the quantum vacuum.
  \item[Warp bubble] Region of distorted spacetime enclosing a vehicle in the
    Alcubierre framework.
  \item[Weak energy condition] The requirement that energy density be
    non-negative for all timelike observers.
\end{description}

\section{Falsification Criteria}\label{app:falsification}

The generic label ``warp bubble'' is not yet a sufficiently specified empirical
hypothesis to produce a unique spectrum, morphology, or medium-interaction
prediction. It becomes falsifiable only when a model states its metric, supporting
stress-energy, formation history, matter coupling, quantum state, observer, and
detector thresholds in advance. Appropriate tests would then include:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Kinematic prediction failure.} A preregistered model must predict
    invariant or observer-specific trajectories with uncertainty, including the
    proper acceleration of the vehicle and occupants. Repeatable calibrated
    measurements outside that prediction would falsify that model. Examples are
    structural deformation or onboard accelerometer readings showing large proper
    acceleration where the model predicts geodesic transport.
  \item \textbf{Curvature prediction failure.} A null result constrains a model
    only when the model predicts a curvature or strain signal above the
    instrument's stated sensitivity and backgrounds.
  \item \textbf{Matter-coupling prediction failure.} Claims of suppressed shock,
    drag, heating, cavitation, or wake require quantitative fluid/matter
    calculations. Observations inconsistent with those preregistered outputs
    would falsify that construction.
  \item \textbf{Radiation prediction failure.} Absence of a band is probative only
    after an explicit quantum-field calculation predicts a spectrum and flux
    above detector thresholds (Sec.~\ref{sec:em-signatures}). There is currently
    no generic DCE or THz prediction to falsify.
  \item \textbf{Source inconsistency.} A proposed metric is physically undermined
    if its source violates its own stated conservation, energy-condition,
    quantum-inequality, causality, formation, or stability requirements.
  \item \textbf{Conventional resolution of the observations.} If calibrated data
    establish ordinary causes for the motivating cases, the evidential motivation
    for applying any warp model to those cases collapses even though the
    mathematical metric remains a valid formal solution.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Note:} No morphology--velocity sequence has been derived, so absence of
such a correlation is not a valid falsification criterion. Tests constrain a
specified physical model, not general relativity or every possible exotic
propulsion proposal.
